# Freeze the pose, mature the rest

The highest-value methods we missed are not another de-novo generator but a **pose-preserving affinity-maturation stack that cannot drift off our His switch** — led by **StaB-ddG** (ProteinMPNN-based binding-ΔΔG, CC-BY, pip-installable, PyRosetta-free, matches FoldX at ~0.2 s/mutation on GPU ([arXiv:2507.05502](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05502v1))) — plus three cheap upgrades that each fix a specific weak link in our current plan: swapping **PROPKA3**, which benchmarks as the *worst* histidine pKa predictor (His R²≈0.05 ([PMC10466379](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466379/))), for a multi-class pKa consensus (**DeepKa + pKAI+ + pKALM/pKa-ANI**); adding **FoldX/mutateX**, the one maturation tool with an explicit pH parameter so affinity and the 7.4-vs-6.0 switch are co-scored ([mutateX 2022](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/23/3/bbac074/6552273)); and replacing SolubleMPNN with **SolubleCaliby**, which recovers good binders SolubleMPNN wrongly rejects on the Adaptyv expression gate ([bioRxiv 2025.09.30.679633](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.30.679633.full.pdf)). The organizing insight is that ArcRefine's carryover (reusing Boltz-2 embeddings across sequence-update steps) is only one point in a broad space, and its *embedding-side, optimization-time* flavor is riskier for a His switch than the family that **mutates the fixed complex in place** (StaB-ddG, GearBind, FoldX, RDE-PPI), where the pose cannot move by construction. Every one of these is pH-blind, so they mature or diversify the pH-7.4 bound state only; the switch must still be gated downstream by our explicit two-state MD and the new multi-pKa consensus. For cross-reactivity the purpose-built tools (**Chamaileon**, **Odin-Multi**) are 2026 preprints with unconfirmed code, so the usable route is a **worst-case `min`-over-orthologs** score across ≥2 folders from Anthropic's **`uplifting-biomolecular-modeling`** hub (Apache-2.0, 36 uv-installable kits ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/uplifting-biomolecular-modeling))), not a single co-fold delta. Adopt four, skip the rest — detailed below.

## Mutate-in-place beats embedding carryover for a frozen switch

ArcRefine's one real trick is *embedding-side, optimization-time* carryover — it seeds a Mosaic optimizer from an existing design plus the bound complex and carries Boltz-2's single and pair representations across sequence-update steps, biasing every gradient update toward the already-found binding mode ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23115832)). That is a *soft* bias: the pose can still drift, which is exactly why its binding-only objective, rewriting ~two-thirds of residues, would erase our engineered His switch. The carryover family we under-cataloged splits into three mechanism classes, and for a His-mediated switch the ordering of risk is the opposite of novelty. **Mutate-in-place ΔΔG predictors carry the pose by construction** — they score point mutations on the frozen complex, so the dock and the His geometry cannot move — making them the cleanest complement to a switch we must not disturb; this class is developed in the affinity-maturation section. **Structure-side carryover** holds a motif, substructure, or backbone fixed and regenerates the rest: RFdiffusion3's built-in partial-diffusion task is the all-atom version ([PMC12458353](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12458353/)), and the flow/diffusion motif-scaffolders (FrameFlow, FoldFlow-2, Genie2, Chroma's substructure conditioner) are the backbone-only versions, several of which fix absolute motif coordinates (RFd3, FrameFlow) while Genie2 conditions coordinate-free, an advantage when the exact rigid placement of the interface motif is uncertain ([Genie2 arXiv:2405.15489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15489)). **Prediction-side carryover** is the cheapest to add and needs no new training: feeding the bound complex as a Boltz-2 or Chai-1 template plus distance/pocket/contact restraints pins the pose at refold — Chai-1's conditioning on just four epitope residues more than doubled prediction success ([Chai-1 restraints](https://blog.samson-connect.net/guided-structure-predictions-with-chai-1-using-pocket-and-contact-restraints)). The flow-matching refiners **PPIFlow** and **AffinityFlow**, and the GA refiner **EvoPro** (whose multi-objective score includes an explicit bound-vs-unbound "conformational stability" anti-drift term ([repo](https://github.com/Kuhlman-Lab/evopro))), are full-pipeline ArcRefine analogs, but PPIFlow and AffinityFlow both re-pack or perturb the interface and therefore risk the same His···Lys erasure as ArcRefine — mature by perturbing the periphery only.

## PXDesign and all-atom generators, not more backbone-only RFd

Our own record is decisive on the generator question: pure de-novo RFdiffusion-1 and BoltzGen floored at af2ig PAE ~22 on TNF-α, and only all-atom RFdiffusion3 broke through — so for a flat/polar antibody-only epitope, **all-atom generation matters more than the specific codebase**. The highest-value missed *open* additions are therefore all-atom and inversion designers, not more backbone-only tools. **PXDesign** (ByteDance's Protenix-design suite) is the standout: it bundles both a diffusion generator and a hallucination generator with structure-confidence filtering and reports **20–73% nanomolar hit rates across five of six diverse targets, surpassing AlphaProteo**, fully open at `github.com/bytedance/PXDesign` ([ByteDance Seed](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/public_papers/pxdesign-fast-modular-and-accurate-de-novo-design-of-protein-binders)). **BoltzDesign1** (MIT) inverts open Boltz weights and usefully supports **partial redesign with <4 Å interface residues held fixed** — a built-in carryover mode ([HF](https://huggingface.co/JoeyCh/BoltzDesign1)). Among all-atom backbone generators that reinforce the RFd3 route, **La-Proteina** adds atomistic motif scaffolding ([NVIDIA](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/genair/publication/laproteina2025/)), **SALAD** adds per-denoising-step output editing as a carryover handle ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.31.635780.full.pdf)), and **Genie3**/**Proteus** extend motif-scaffolding backbones. The antibody/CDR generators (**Chai-2**, **Germinal**, **RFantibody**, **IgDesign**) are mechanistically best-suited to flat polar epitopes because they build concave CDR paratopes, and **AlphaProteo's ~700× improvement on IL-17A** is the closest public precedent that a generator can crack a trimeric inflammatory cytokine ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240906-google-deepmind-alphaproteo/)) — but they output Cys-containing VHH/scFv formats, conflicting with the 0-Cys 60–120 aa mini-binder spec, so use them to mine binding modes, not as drop-in generators. **ProteinGenerator** deserves a note: its amino-acid composition biasing is a direct lever for 0-Cys / polar-surface constraints ([bioRxiv](https://biorxiv.org/cgi/content/short/2023.09.11.556673v1)).

## PROPKA3 is the weakest link in our switch

The most consequential finding for objective #1 is that our pH-selectivity physics currently rests on the benchmark-*worst* histidine pKa predictor. In the head-to-head JCIM 2023 benchmark of seven methods on 41 histidines, **PROPKA3 scored RMSE 1.20 and R²≈0.05 ("no clear advantage"), while DeepKa led the empirical class (RMSE 0.89, R²0.43), with H++ and pKAI+ next (RMSE 0.94)**, and a simple DeepKa+PROPKA3 arithmetic consensus was best overall (RMSE 0.76) ([PMC10466379](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466379/)). This directly operationalizes our standing "sign-consensus across method-classes" lesson with concrete uv-installable tools: **pKAI/pKAI+** (`pip install pkai`, continuum-electrostatics ML ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pkai))), **PypKa** (Poisson–Boltzmann, `pip install pypka` ([docs](https://pypka.readthedocs.io/))), **pKALM** (sequence-only PLM, RMSE 0.83, 4,961 pKa/sec ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613101.full.pdf))), and **pKa-ANI** (QM-flavored ANI-2x atomic environments ([PMC8864681](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8864681/))) — compute the Wyman integral with each and keep the His sign-consensus, flagging any case where predictors disagree by >~0.5 pKa. Beyond pKa, the conditional-design landscape offers alternatives but no drop-in pH generator: **protease-activated de-novo peptide masks** give >100–1000× OFF/ON but are protease-gated, not pH-recycling, so they change the product profile rather than satisfy the pH-6.0 spec ([PMC12703739](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12703739/)); **de-novo two-state hinges, LOCKR/Co-LOCKR, and LucCage** give true two-state selectivity but hit our monolithic-fold buildability wall (~1/512) for a compact mini-binder, so keep LucCage only as a wet-lab biosensor readout idea ([Science 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10697137/); [LucCage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/8085813/)). The buried-His filament work (sharp reversible transition over 0.3 pH units with 6–9 buried His) is the design precedent for cooperative multi-His switches ([Nat. Nanotechnol. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41565-024-01641-1)). Crucially, open constant-pH MD remains a NO-GO in our environment (Chodera's `protons` unreleased, Force Field X is Java, Amber/CHARMM licensed), and Rosetta multistate `ProteinInterfaceMS` is PyRosetta-blocked — both vindicating our explicit fixed-protonation two-state MD + xTB as the right open path, with no drop-in substitute to switch to.

## StaB-ddG ranks affinity without touching the pose

For objective #3, a mature set of fast ML binding-ΔΔG predictors now operates on a **fixed complex backbone** — the purest pose-preserving maturation, because the mutation is applied in place and the dock cannot drift. **StaB-ddG** is the single best fit: pure deep learning on ProteinMPNN embeddings, it needs neither PyRosetta nor FoldX, enforces thermodynamic antisymmetry, is the first DL predictor to match force-field FoldX on binding ΔΔG (SKEMPI Spearman 0.45, **0.53 ensembled with FoldX**), runs at ~0.2 s/mutation on GPU, and ships CC-BY at `github.com/LDeng0205/StaB-ddG` ([arXiv:2507.05502](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05502v1)). **GearBind** adds an independent, wet-lab-validated signal (geometric GNN; raised CR3022–Omicron affinity up to 17× and a human VHH up to 5.6× ([Nat. Commun. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/326a856cdb144984ae87d5a5ddcdd218))), and **BA-DDG** explicitly models the unbound state in the thermodynamic cycle (SKEMPI 0.51 supervised, but code unverified ([arXiv:2410.09543](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09543v1))). The physics leg, **FoldX/mutateX**, is uniquely valuable here because its explicit pH parameter lets a single run check that a maturation mutation does not flatten the 7.4-vs-6.0 ΔΔG gap that encodes the His switch — maturation and switch-preservation co-scored ([mutateX](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/23/3/bbac074/6552273)). **ThermoMPNN** and **ProteinMPNN-ddG** are *stability* predictors, not binding, so they belong as a developability guardrail (reject interface mutations that destabilize the monomer), not as the affinity signal ([ThermoMPNN](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10402116/)). The loop that ties these together is **EvoProtGrad** (MIT): gradient-MCMC directed evolution wrapping any fitness as a black box, so wrapping StaB-ddG and restricting moves to non-switch interface positions gives a pose-preserving, PyRosetta-free maturation loop ([arXiv:2212.09925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09925v1)); **ALLM-Ab** is the developability-aware active-learning template if a richer loop is wanted ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.05.668775.full.pdf)). Because the Oct-2025 "Mapping the evolution of computationally designed protein binders" analysis finds affinity-increasing mutations are largely additive and often sit *away* from the direct interface ([bioRxiv 2025.10.04.680454](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.04.680454)), this pairs naturally with holding the interface and switch fixed while sweeping the rest. For cross-reactivity the practical configuration is to run the ΔΔG ensemble on both the human-TNF and mouse-TNF complexes and rank by `min(ΔΔG_human, ΔΔG_mouse)`, rejecting any mutation that helps one species but hurts the other — no tool does dual-ortholog maturation out of the box.

## One Apache-2.0 hub plus worst-case ortholog scoring

The biggest ecosystem miss is Anthropic's own **`uplifting-biomolecular-modeling`** — 36 Apache-2.0, uv-installable, H100-optimized kits that both *map* the sanctioned 2025–26 SOTA toolchain (AF3, Boltz-2, Chai-1, RFdiffusion3, BoltzGen, PXDesign, Proteina-Complexa, Genie3, Caliby, ESM-C, E1) and *accelerate* it with `exact`/`fast`/`big` speed modes and SHA-256-verified weights, matching our uv/https constraint exactly ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/uplifting-biomolecular-modeling)). If a kit exists for a tool, that tool is in-scope for this competition — and the kits to pull that we are likely not running are **caliby** (expression), **ef2inv/pxdesign/complexa/genie3** (orthogonal generators), and **esmc/e1** (sequence-plausibility scorers Adaptyv's post-mortem ranked above iPAE). From it, the concrete expression win is **SolubleCaliby**: Protpardelle-1c binder designs deemed undesignable by SolubleMPNN are designable under SolubleCaliby, exposing SolubleMPNN's filter limitations ([bioRxiv 2025.09.30.679633](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.30.679633.full.pdf)) — decisive because expression is Adaptyv's hard gate and our mini-binder pool is small. For the human↔mouse cross-reactivity objective there is now a dedicated literature: **Chamaileon** unifies multi-state and multi-target design to optimize one sequence across contexts via decoupled noise schedules and mixture-of-paths sampling ([arXiv:2607.23518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23518)), **Odin-Multi** optimizes one binder against multiple complexes while *discouraging* off-targets, and **DynamicMPNN** is the first explicit multi-state inverse-folding model (+25% decoy-normalized RMSD over ProteinMPNN-MSD, but trained on conformations not orthologs ([arXiv:2507.21938](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21938v2))). All three lack confirmed, runnable code, so the usable route today is the cheapest: a **worst-case two-complex loss** (`max(L_human, L_mouse)`) inside our Mosaic/BindCraft stack over the conserved epitope, plus a scoring upgrade — co-fold vs human and mouse TNF with ≥2 folders, compute **ipSAE_min per ortholog**, and rank by the *worst* ortholog ([ipSAE](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11844409/)); worst-case beats averaging, which lets a strong human term mask a failing mouse term. Because the antibody literature shows cross-reactivity is governed by epitope conservation ([IL-6 cross-react patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8198414)), the single cheapest high-value "score" is deterministic and folder-independent: overlay the designed contact map on the human/mouse TNF alignment and reject any design whose energetic hotspots sit on non-conserved positions.

## Five claims to distrust before you cite them

Five things in this literature are unverified or actively misleading, and each would corrupt a decision if taken at face value. **(1) The "multitarget-AF2 → KIT (mouse+human) and HA-stalk (H1/H3/H5) cross-reactive" precedent is unverifiable.** It appeared only in AI-generated WebSearch summaries and was confirmed *absent* from every primary source checked — AlphaProteo (arXiv 2409.08022), BindCraft (2024.09.30.615802), and Bennett's UW thesis — most likely a summarizer mis-attributing a Chamaileon/Odin-Multi benchmark case; do not cite KIT/HA as a cross-reactivity precedent without verifying against Chamaileon's CROSS benchmark ([arXiv:2607.23518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23518)). **(2) ArcRefine is a single-author, non-peer-reviewed Zenodo preprint** (Ken Osumi, FoldArc Co., 2026-10-05) with no arXiv/bioRxiv/PubMed index; every wet-lab number — 6/10 BLI-confirmed TNF-α binders, ipSAE 0.49→0.69 — is self-reported and vendor-validated, and a "13 nM" affinity quoted in some briefs does not appear in the Zenodo abstract and must not be cited as established ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23115832)). **(3) PROPKA3 is the benchmark-worst histidine pKa predictor** (His R²≈0.05 ([PMC10466379](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466379/))) — our Wyman linkage currently rests on exactly this method, so treat its His pKa as one weak vote in a consensus, never as ground truth. **(4) The Boltz-2 affinity head is weak on protein–protein**: it approaches FEP accuracy for small molecules (~0.6 on FEP+) but scores Pearson 0.15 / Spearman 0.09 on protein–protein TCR3d, *worse than an ESM2 sequence baseline* (0.239) ([PMC12262699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12262699/)), so use Boltz-2 only for pose/ipTM confidence and never as the affinity ranker for a protein mini-binder. **(5) The strongest 2026 methods have unconfirmed code or licenses** — PPIFlow (CC-BY-NC, no repo cited, and a non-standard `10.64898` DOI prefix to verify), AffinityFlow (code "after acceptance"), Chamaileon, Odin-Multi, DynamicMPNN, ZetaDial, TorchCraft, BA-DDG, and Twin Peaks are all "verify install first-hand" before any reliance, and ODesign/La-Proteina carry webserver-first / NVIDIA-non-commercial friction. A secondary caveat: ML ΔΔG predictors' reported edge over Flex ddG can be inflated by train/test data leakage, so trust them for *ranking small perturbations*, not absolute de-novo interface quality.

## Adopt four, skip the rest

Spend the remaining three days adopting exactly four additions, each closing a named gap in the existing playbook and split cleanly across the three objectives. **For switch-hardening (objective #1), replace PROPKA3-only Wyman with a multi-class pKa consensus** (DeepKa + pKAI+ + pKALM or pKa-ANI) and add a **FoldX pH-dependent ΔΔG(7.4 vs 6.0)** cross-check so every matured sequence is re-scored for the switch, not just affinity — this is hours of `pip` work that fixes the single weakest quantity in our pipeline, and our explicit two-state MD remains the judge. **For affinity maturation (objective #3), adopt the StaB-ddG + GearBind + FoldX ensemble** scanning non-switch interface positions on the frozen backbone, gated for monomer stability by ThermoMPNN and wrapped in an **EvoProtGrad** loop; this is pose-preserving by construction (no His drift), PyRosetta-free, and the cleanest in-class complement to ArcRefine's trick. **For expression (the hard gate), swap in SolubleCaliby** via the `caliby` kit as the final re-sequence. **For cross-reactivity scoring (objective #2), adopt the worst-case recipe** — `min(ipSAE_min_human, ipSAE_min_mouse)` across ≥2 uplifting-kit folders plus a deterministic contact-conservation check — rather than trusting one folder's absolute cross-species ipTM. Skip, deliberately: ArcRefine itself (policy-denied, PolyForm Noncommercial, switch-erasing objective); PPIFlow and AffinityFlow (unconfirmed code and backbone-perturbing, so they risk the His geometry); the allosteric/hinge/LOCKR switches (monolithic-fold buildability wall) and protease masks (wrong product profile); the Boltz-2 affinity head as a ranker; Chamaileon/Odin-Multi until a runnable repo lands; and the closed Chai-2/AlphaProteo.

## Conclusion

The reframe this survey forces is that the carryover family's *most novel* members are its *riskiest* for our problem: ArcRefine's embedding bias, PPIFlow's re-noising, and AffinityFlow's inverse-fold loop all let the interface — and therefore the His switch — move, whereas the humble mutate-in-place ΔΔG predictors (StaB-ddG, GearBind, FoldX) carry the pose by construction and are the right engine for a design whose selectivity is set residue-by-residue. The genuinely new, usable wins are small and surgical, not a new generator: a pKa consensus that retires our weakest predictor, a pH-aware physics ΔΔG that co-scores the switch, an expression model that beats the one gating our pool, and a worst-case ortholog score that fixes folder pessimism. Against that, the field's loudest 2026 results split into two buckets that both resist adoption — the closed frontier models (Chai-2, AlphaProteo) we cannot run, and the open preprints (Chamaileon, PPIFlow, ODesign, TorchCraft) whose code or license is unconfirmed — leaving the durable lever exactly where the earlier playbook put it: mature and diversify the pH-7.4 pose with pose-preserving tools we own and can verify, then let our own two-state physics, not any single predictor, decide whether the switch survived.
