# Reproduce ArcRefine in-house, score like Adaptyv

ArcRefine is exactly the method we missed — a code-first Boltz-2 + Mosaic "structural-carryover" refiner that turned **1/10 into 6/10 Adaptyv-BLI-confirmed TNF-α binders for ~$677** ([foldarc.com](https://foldarc.com/research/structural-carryover/); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23115832)) — but we should not run it. Its install was policy-denied as external code, its license is PolyForm Noncommercial, and, decisively, its objective is **binding-only and rewrites roughly two-thirds of residues with no pH or cross-reactivity term**, which would erase the engineered His switch we are building. The right move is to reproduce its single real trick — seed the optimizer from our current binder and carry Boltz-2's single/pair embeddings across sequence updates — inside our own **MIT-licensed Mosaic** ([escalante-bio/mosaic](https://github.com/escalante-bio/mosaic)), under a multi-state objective that fixes the His switch and the Region-I hotspots. The higher-value finding is competition intelligence: Adaptyv's open `protein-qc` repo discloses the **exact gates** designs face (ipSAE_min >0.61, PAE_interaction <12, shape complementarity >0.50, interface_dG < −10, plus hard expression filters), and the published EGFR post-mortem proves that **iPAE/ipTM are weak rankers (ROC ≈ 0.64)** while ESM-C/ESM3 log-likelihood (0.72), interface ΔG, and **SolubleMPNN-driven expression (93% vs ~0%)** decide who wins ([bioRxiv 2025.04.17.648362](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.17.648362v1.full)). So rank by a composite, gate expression first, submit diversified variants of any binder that already works alongside pure de-novo, and plan the switch as a two-pH BLI readout. To crack the flat-polar affinity wall itself, point **β-pairing targeted RFdiffusion** (runs on our RFdiffusion, ~9× hit-rate lift on immunoglobulin β-sandwich folds) and **Proteina-Complexa** (open all-atom, ~10 s/design) at the conserved hydrophobic **Region-I cluster (Val74/Leu75/Ile97 + Trp114/Tyr115)**, the epitope that gave sub-nM minibinders with automatic human/mouse cross-reactivity ([PMC12644665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12644665/)). This report owns the methods/tools/competition layer; the switch-chemistry and epitope biology are developed in the companion "Cooperative histidine networks" report and are referenced here only where they constrain a tool choice.

## ArcRefine is real, un-runnable here, and its objective would erase our switch

ArcRefine is a verified but preliminary artifact, and understanding precisely what it is settles how to use it. The GitHub repo `ken-osumi/ArcRefine` exists; the backing document is a **single-author, non-peer-reviewed Zenodo preprint (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23115832, dated 2026-10-05)** by Ken Osumi of FoldArc Co., Ltd., a one-person commercial binder-design vendor ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23115832); [foldarc.com](https://foldarc.com)). Every wet-lab number is self-reported in that preprint. Mechanically, ArcRefine is **not a new model but a thin fork of Mosaic**: its own README states it is "built on Mosaic, with structural carryover added to its optimization loop," it bundles the required Mosaic modules, and it retains the Mosaic command names "for compatibility" ([ArcRefine README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ken-osumi/ArcRefine/main/README.md)). The entire novelty is two moves — seed the optimizer from an existing design plus the target complex, and **carry Boltz-2's single and pair representations across sequence-update steps** (a warm start that biases every update toward the already-found binding mode) — followed by a final independent Boltz-2 prediction without the carried state as an honesty check. Ken Osumi's other repos are all forks (RFdiffusion, ColabDesign, Protenix, a `mosaic-fork`, Prefect), confirming the lineage generate→gradient-refine→fold with no second original method ([github.com/ken-osumi](https://github.com/ken-osumi)).

The self-reported results are genuinely on-target: ArcRefine modified roughly two-thirds of residues while retaining the binding interface in **23 of 60 TNF-α and 45 of 60 PD-L1 designs**, lifted median ipSAE from **0.49 to 0.69** across six predictors, and yielded **six BLI-confirmed TNF-α binders (vs 1/10 unoptimized) for ~$677 of compute**, validated by Adaptyv's own BLI ([foldarc.com](https://foldarc.com/research/structural-carryover/); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23115832)). The author is honest that the results "do not isolate the effect of structural carryover alone or establish that binding occurs at the predicted site."

Three independent reasons make running ArcRefine the wrong call, and none of them requires relitigating the others. First, **its install was policy-denied as external code**, so the fast path in the raw tool survey is closed to us. Second, the license is **PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0** — fine for academic research but a flag we avoid when an MIT-licensed equivalent exists ([ArcRefine README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ken-osumi/ArcRefine/main/README.md)). Third and most important, **ArcRefine optimizes a binding-only objective and rewrites ~two-thirds of the sequence with no pH term and no cross-reactivity term.** We are engineering a His switch whose selectivity is set residue-by-residue and an epitope chosen for human/mouse conservation; an optimizer blind to both will, by construction, drift the sequence off the switch and off the conserved contacts, and our own record shows that wholesale re-sequencing of a backbone whose own sequence binds folds beautifully yet kills binding. The verdict is therefore not "ArcRefine is bad" but "ArcRefine's *trick* is good and its *objective* is wrong for us" — so we take the trick into a tool we own, which is the next section.

## Own the refiner: Mosaic under a multi-state objective, never a binding-only one

The ArcRefine-class refiner family is the set of methods that improve a binder *sequence* by running a structure predictor in the loop while trying to hold the binding mode. We already own its strongest member. **Mosaic** (Escalante Bio, MIT, already installed) does gradient-based optimization over a continuous relaxed sequence space, composing multiple expert models — **Boltz-1/2, AlphaFold2, OpenFold3, Protenix, ESMFold** — into one differentiable loss, with ProteinMPNN sequence recovery inside the loop ([Mosaic](https://github.com/escalante-bio/mosaic)). It is the exact engine ArcRefine extends, it is Boltz-2-native like our stack, and on the Adaptyv Nipah de-novo target it reported **89% hit-rate (8 binders / 9 designs)** ([Adaptyv skill writeup](https://www.skills.sh/adaptyvbio/protein-design-skills/mosaic)). Because Mosaic exposes configurable recycling and MPNN recovery, reproducing ArcRefine's carryover is cheap: seed the logits from our current TNF-α binder, raise `recycling_steps`, bias or freeze the trunk representation across steps, and keep the interface residues fixed — a warm start that approximates cross-step embedding carryover without ArcRefine's code or license. Crucially, Mosaic's multi-model loss is the right substrate for a **custom pH-switch objective**: a bind-at-pH-7.4 term + a repulsion-at-pH-6.0 term + a SolubleMPNN/expression term, summed over human and mouse TNF, in one differentiable pass — something ArcRefine's fixed single-mode carryover cannot encode.

The other-group members of this class are backups, not upgrades. **BoltzDesign1** ([yehlincho/BoltzDesign1](https://github.com/yehlincho/BoltzDesign1); [bioRxiv 2025.04.06.647261](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.06.647261v1)) is the direct differentiable-Boltz analog of ArcRefine from another group — it inverts Boltz by gradient ascent on logits and, usefully, supports **partial redesign with interface residues (<4 Å) held fixed**, which is the closest built-in "refine existing binder" mode. Its license is the one real caution: GitHub's API detected no standard LICENSE file, and although a secondary summary claims MIT, that is unverified — read the LICENSE before any non-research use, and note it defaults to single-chain/Boltz-1 and is "not experimentally validated." **EvoPro** ([Kuhlman-Lab/evopro](https://github.com/Kuhlman-Lab/evopro); [PMC10710080](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10710080/)) is a genetic-algorithm optimizer over AF2 that scores a placement-confidence term, a fold-confidence term, and — relevant to us — a **bound-vs-unbound conformational-stability term**, i.e. it already rewards a consistent bound pose; it is compute-heavy (many AF2 calls per generation, which maps well to 8×H100) and preserves the mode only if constrained. **BindCraft** (MIT, needs PyRosetta) is de-novo AF2 hallucination rather than a refiner, but its `predict_initial_guess`/`predict_bigbang` pose-biasing is the generation-side analog of binding-mode preservation.

The one hard constraint governs all of them and is worth stating as a rule: **binding-mode, pH-switch, and cross-reactivity preservation are usage constraints, not free properties.** Every generative optimizer here — Mosaic, BoltzDesign1, EvoPro, BindCraft/MPNN — will drift off the current binding mode unless you (a) fix the His switch residue(s) and the validated hotspots, (b) restrict the mutable set to non-switch interface positions, and (c) make the objective **multi-state**: sum losses over human-TNF + mouse-TNF and over pH 7.4 (bound) + pH 6.0 (released). The safest maturation is therefore not aggressive full-interface re-design but **point-mutation scanning on the fixed backbone** — PPIformer or GearBind to short-list affinity-improving mutations at non-switch positions, each confirmed by re-folding with AF2-IG and holding the switch ([PPIformer](https://github.com/anton-bushuiev/PPIformer); [GearBind](https://github.com/DeepGraphLearning/GearBind)). This multi-state constraint is a design recommendation, not a benchmarked result — no source quantifies how well any optimizer preserves a His-mediated pH switch — which is exactly why move #2's composite re-score at both pH states and both species sits downstream of every refinement run.

## β-pairing RFdiffusion and Proteina-Complexa hit Region-I to beat the flat-polar wall

Refinement cannot rescue a binder that never formed; the generator frontier matters because TNF-α's flat polar β-jellyroll face is where de-novo design has repeatedly floored. Two newly surveyed, code-available generators change the odds, and both should be pointed at the same epitope. The mechanistically matched tool is **β-pairing targeted RFdiffusion** (Baker, Nat Commun 2025), purpose-built for "therapeutically relevant polar targets" with exposed, unpaired edge β-strands — explicitly including **immunoglobulin-fold β-sandwich domains** (FCRL5, KIT, PDGFRα), structurally the same family as TNF-α's β-jellyroll ([bioRxiv 2024.10.11.617496](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.11.617496.full.pdf)). It conditions RFdiffusion via secondary-structure and block-adjacency (SS/ADJ) tensors so a binder β-strand forms an extended β-sheet pairing with a chosen target edge strand — a β-augmentation binding mode in which backbone-backbone H-bonds, not a flat polar surface, carry the interaction. The payoff is large and measured: **>9.2% of β-strand-conditioned designs passed quality metrics versus 0.98% for hotspot-conditioned RFdiffusion (~9×)**, affinities spanned **137 pM to mid-nM** (ALK-3bp 528 pM, PDGFRαbp 137 pM), a KIT co-crystal (**PDB 9H71, 2.8 Å**) sat 1.9 Å RMSD from the design, and all-by-all SPR showed **no off-target binding even among homologous family members** — directly relevant to our need to discriminate TNF-α cleanly. Because it rides on the SS/ADJ machinery already in our RFdiffusion install (co-author David E. Kim), it is implementable within the deadline by scripting the (L,T)→tensor mapping; the one gap is that the turnkey strand-pairing conditioning script was not located as a standalone repo, so budget time to rebuild it from the paper's supplement or RFdiffusion helper scripts.

The newest open all-atom generator is **Proteina-Complexa** (NVIDIA + ManifoldBio, ICLR 2026 Oral), a fully-atomistic flow-matching model combining large-scale generative pretraining with inference-time search; it reports "markedly higher in-silico success rates" than RFdiffusion and that its test-time search "greatly outperforms" BindCraft hallucination on a 19-target benchmark ([alphaXiv 2603.27950](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.27950); [NVIDIA GenAIR](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/genair/proteina-complexa)). Weights are open on GitHub/HuggingFace/NGC under the NVIDIA Open Model License (planned Apache-2.0) and it runs at **~9–10 s/design** on A100-class hardware, so a multi-thousand-design TNF-α campaign fits inside two days on 8×H100 ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Iambackup/NV-Proteina-Complexa-Protein-Target-160M-v1)). It places explicit side chains at the interface — the right class for a polar target — and complements the RFd3 all-atom breakthrough already in our record. (SeedProteo, the one 2026 generator to score an in-silico TNF-α success, is **webserver-only** and a poor fit for our local, no-outbound-except-https setting; BoltzGen and BoltzDesign1 are cheap reuse-our-Boltz add-ons but add little over our existing stack.)

Both generators should be aimed at the same place: the conserved hydrophobic **Region-I cleft — Val74/Leu75/Ile97 on one monomer plus Trp114/Tyr115 on the adjacent monomer** — not the flat polar face. This epitope is the one that yielded sub-nanomolar de-novo minibinders (TMB-1 at **0.55 nM**, cryo-EM **PDB 9JEC**) built with classical Rosetta RIF, and targeting it **automatically buys human/mouse cross-reactivity** because it deliberately avoids the non-conserved D-E loop and the design inhibited TNF-α-driven osteoclastogenesis in mouse BMDMs ([PMC12644665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12644665/)). The strategic reframe — which the companion switch-chemistry report develops in full — is that "unbindable TNF-α" attaches to the *epitope* (the flat polar subunit-interface patch that AlphaProteo explicitly failed on), not the molecule: Region-I is bindable, conserved, and the correct target spec for every generator we run.

## Baker's pH-filter and HBNet add cheap PyRosetta layers; our two-state physics stays the judge

For the conditional layer, the field has exactly one experimentally-validated, direction-correct (bind 7.4 / release low-pH) de-novo mini-binder pipeline — **Baker 2025** — and its code is not released, so the actionable move is to port its recipe rather than wait for a drop. Baker's pipeline is RFdiffusion backbones → ProteinMPNN with **histidine bias (0.5/1.0/1.5) and explicit donor/acceptor specification** (naive His enrichment gave "little pH-sensitivity") → AF2 filter (pae_interaction <8, pLDDT >88) → selection on a burial-weighted pH_score, a `fa_elec`-only **ΔΔG_elec(pH5−pH7) ≥ 0** filter, and enumerated His↔cationic-residue distances ([PMC12621740](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12621740/)). Two pieces transfer immediately and cheaply onto our existing RFd3/MPNN output. The **`fa_elec` pH5-vs-pH7 ΔΔG filter plus the His↔cation distance script** is a high-throughput geometric/electrostatic pre-filter — a PyRosetta reimplementation that ranks thousands of candidates in roughly a day — and its validation pedigree is the best available for our target class: it drove **TNFR2 to 122× weaker at low pH** on exactly our generator family. The key integration point, which the tool survey makes explicit, is that **Baker ran no pKa predictor in the loop** and relied on the textbook His pKa ~6.5; our PROPKA-Wyman linkage + JustHISpKa (His MUE ~0.4) + explicit two-state MD is strictly more rigorous on the one quantity that decides the switch — the *effective in-complex* pKa — so Baker's filter ranks the many and our physics judges the tail.

The structural installer for a *sharper* switch is **HBNet** (Rosetta/PyRosetta; Boyken et al.), which exhaustively enumerates closed buried hydrogen-bond networks, scores them, and seeds them into a scaffold with constraints; it is open (RosettaCommons / PyRosetta free for academics) with a worked notebook, and it is the documented way to position "pH-responsive hydrogen bond networks in the core of protein bundles" ([HBNetMover docs](https://docs.rosettacommons.org/docs/latest/scripting_documentation/RosettaScripts/Movers/movers_pages/HBNetMover); [PyRosetta notebook 06.05](https://nbviewer.org/github/RosettaCommons/PyRosetta.notebooks/blob/master/notebooks/06.05-HBNet-Before-Design.ipynb)). This is the tool that implements the cooperative multi-His mechanism the companion report argues for; the one design constraint to carry over from Baker's data is the empirical **≥2 protonated-His↔cation contact rule** — every working pH-dependent binder had two or more, random single-His incorporation failed, and monomer-destabilization used 2–3-residue buried networks. Adopt that rule as a hard gate. The rest of the conditional-tool landscape is thin for us: **SIpHAB** (NRC, free) is direction-correct for acidic release but antibody-Fv-sequence-only, a triage predictor usable only if we frame a binder as a VHH/scFv ([runner](https://mm.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/software/siphab/runner)); **AlloGen/TD3B** (ChatterjeeLab, code+checkpoints released) is the strongest released state-selective generator but targets agonist/antagonist, is not pH-aware, and states no license ([HF ChatterjeeLab/TD3B](https://huggingface.co/ChatterjeeLab/TD3B)); and for the pKa consensus, adding **DeepKa** (cpHMD-trained, the benchmarked His partner to PROPKA3) and keeping **pKAI** (`pip install pKAI`) are hours-scale upgrades to the sign-consensus we already run ([pKa benchmark PMC10466379](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466379/); [pKAI](https://pypi.org/project/pkai)). Constant-pH MD tooling (GROMACS+phbuilder, Amber pmemd, Force Field X) is real but remains a NO-GO in our uv/https environment; our fixed-protonation explicit two-state MD substitutes.

## Adaptyv's open QC repo and EGFR post-mortem reveal exactly how designs get judged

This is the highest-value intelligence in the report, because the organizer has effectively published the scoring function. Adaptyv's **`adaptyvbio/protein-design-skills`** repo (24 MIT-licensed, Modal-GPU-wrapped Claude skills) is a curated catalog of the sanctioned toolchain with the QC thresholds baked in ([GitHub](https://github.com/adaptyvbio/protein-design-skills)). Mirroring its `protein-qc` gates aligns our in-silico pre-screen with how designs are actually judged:

| Metric (standard / stringent) | Expression (hard gate) | Liability flags |
|---|---|---|
| ipTM >0.50 / >0.60 · **PAE_interaction <12 / <10** | instability index <40 / <30 | even Cys count |
| **ipSAE_min >0.61 / >0.70** · pLDDT >0.85 / >0.90 | GRAVY <0.4 / <0.2 | flag NG/NS/NT (deamidation) |
| shape complementarity >0.50 / >0.62 | ESM2 PLL >0.0 / >0.2 | ≥3 consecutive K/R (proteolysis) |
| interface_dG < −10 / < −15 · scRMSD <2.0 / <1.5 | folding ΔG < −2 / < −4 kcal/mol | hydrophobic runs ≥6 (aggregation) |

The repo is explicit that **"individual metrics alone are too weak — use composite scoring,"** and even names a weighting (30% pLDDT + 20% ipTM + 20% PAE + 15% SC + 15% sequence-plausibility) and campaign-health bands (>15% pass = excellent, 10–15% good, 5–10% marginal, <5% poor) ([protein-qc SKILL.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/adaptyvbio/protein-design-skills@main/skills/protein-qc/SKILL.md)). Its `ipsae` skill confirms **ipSAE "separates true from false predicted complexes more reliably than ipTM,"** with a **1.4× average-precision gain over the ipAE** that RFdiffusion pipelines commonly filter on, reading AF2/AF3/Boltz-1 output directly ([ipsae SKILL.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/adaptyvbio/protein-design-skills@main/skills/ipsae/SKILL.md)).

The published EGFR post-mortem supplies the empirical backing and the sharpest lesson ([*Crowdsourced Protein Design*, bioRxiv 2025.04.17.648362](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.17.648362v1.full)). The assay is **cell-free protein expression → Bio-Layer Interferometry, ≥2 replicates, Kd from a concentration series** — so expression is a literal gate before binding is ever measured. The ranking metrics most designers trust are weak: **iPAE ROC 0.64, ipTM 0.64, pLDDT 0.55–0.66**, and iPAE correlated with affinity only at Pearson −0.25, such that **the top-2 Round-1 binders ranked 54th and 89th on the iPAE leaderboard**. What actually separated binders was **ESM2 log-likelihood (0.72, best single; ESM-C/ESM3 stronger)**, interface pDockQ/mpDockQ (~0.68), favorable binding ΔG (p<0.001), more interface contacts (p<0.001), and higher H-bond fraction (p=0.004). And the decisive expression finding: **SolubleMPNN gave 93.1% expression vs ~0% for standard ProteinMPNN — "use SolubleMPNN as a final check before submitting"** ([Adaptyv substack](https://adaptyvbio.substack.com/p/protein-optimization-102-lessons)). The winners embody the playbook: Round-1 first place was BindCraft (Kd 491 nM); second place ranked 54th by iPAE but used SolubleMPNN for expression; Round-2 was won by **Cradle's lead-optimized Cetuximab-scFv (1.21 nM, 8.2× over Cetuximab)** — diversifying an existing binder, not pure de-novo ([Cradle blog](https://www.cradle.bio/blog/adaptyv-protein-design-competition)). The Nipah challenge, the closest completed hard-target precedent, saw RFdiffusion take the highest hit rate, validation by SPR, and ~9.3% binding ([Proteinbase/Manifold](https://manifold.markets/Proteinbase)).

For the current Anthropic × Adaptyv 2026 program, final selection runs through an **undisclosed multi-metric Claude workflow that "will not rely on one in silico metric,"** with results (including failures) published openly on Proteinbase on Dec 15 2026 ([intuitionlabs guide](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/anthropic-adaptyv-protein-design-competition-guide)). Two implications follow directly. A design that is **defensible across many orthogonal metrics** — fold confidence, interface quality, expression, sequence plausibility, and a clear mechanistic pH story — is favored over one that maxes a single number. And the pH switch will be read as a **two-pH BLI** (Adaptyv's binding-characterization skill handles buffer pH and covers both BLI and SPR), so the engineered selectivity must survive the composite and be measurable as associate-at-7.4 / dissociate-at-6.0. One standing intelligence asset is underused: **Proteinbase is an open, per-design labeled dataset of what actually expressed and bound in this exact assay** across past cytokine-class targets — mine it directly rather than inferring from literature. The exact TNF-α Challenge-2 rubric and pH/assay spec live inside the Proteinbase app and are not in public prose; the project brief's requirements (bind 7.4 / release 6.0, mouse cross-react, affinity) are authoritative, but verify the exact pH, temperature, and buffer against the live challenge page.

## Calibration: licenses, unverified wet-lab numbers, and the expression-versus-binding tension

Three calibrations keep this plan honest. On **licenses and code-availability**, the owned, clean tools are Mosaic (MIT), BindCraft and dl_binder_design/AF2-IG (MIT), PXDesign and Germinal (Apache-2.0), PPIformer (MIT), GearBind (Apache-2.0), de_novo_binder_scoring (MIT, needs PyRosetta), and the Adaptyv skills (MIT); the flags are ArcRefine (PolyForm Noncommercial, and policy-denied to install regardless), **BoltzDesign1 (no standard LICENSE detected by the GitHub API — a secondary claim of MIT is unverified; read the file before non-research use)**, ColabDesign (NOASSERTION) and EvoBind (no license), Proteina-Complexa (NVIDIA Open Model License, planned Apache-2.0), and AlloGen/TD3B (none stated). Baker's pH-filter and the TNF-α Region-I minibinder protocol are **method-available but code-NOT-released** — reimplement from the papers.

On **unverified numbers**, treat every ArcRefine wet-lab figure as a self-reported, non-peer-reviewed claim from a single-author preprint validated by the vendor's own campaign; the "6/10" and ipSAE "0.49→0.69" are sourced to foldarc.com, but a "13 nM" affinity that appears in some briefs was not found in the Zenodo abstract and should not be cited as established. Generator hit rates (Proteina-Complexa's benchmark wins, β-pairing's ~9×, Mosaic's 89% Nipah) are reported in-silico or on non-TNF targets and have not been reproduced on our TNF-α/pH task. Hard per-design wall-clock and VRAM are unpublished for Mosaic, BoltzDesign1, and ArcRefine; only BindCraft (≥32 GB VRAM, hundreds–thousands of trajectories) and Proteina-Complexa (~10 s/design) are concrete. And the learned ΔΔG regressors (PPIformer, GearBind) are trained on SKEMPI/PPIRef **natural** complexes, so they are trustworthy for ranking small perturbations around an already-good interface but **not** as an absolute de-novo interface-quality score — keep AF2-IG/ipSAE as the primary triage, consistent with the confirmed finding that **Boltz-2's affinity head does not transfer to protein-protein** ([arXiv 2512.06592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06592)).

The subtlest trap is the **expression-versus-binding MPNN tension**. SolubleMPNN is decisive for the expression gate (93% vs ~0%), but it optimizes surface solubility, not interface affinity, and standard ProteinMPNN may produce a tighter interface; layering our own non-negotiable constraints — fix the His switch, fix the Region-I hotspots, stay multi-state over species and pH — further narrows what any MPNN can propose. These objectives genuinely compete, and there is no single sequence that maximizes all of them. The resolution is procedural and already in the playbook: generate candidates, run SolubleMPNN as the **final** re-sequence with the switch and hotspots frozen, then let the Adaptyv-mirrored **composite** — not any one metric — arbitrate between expression and interface quality, re-scored at both pH states and against both human and mouse TNF. A design that cannot survive that composite is not submission-ready no matter how good its single best number looks.

## Conclusion

The actionable reframe is that ArcRefine taught us a technique and a venue, not a tool to run: the technique (seed-from-existing-binder + Boltz-2 embedding carryover) belongs in our own MIT Mosaic under a multi-state objective that defends the switch, and the venue lesson — that the decisive 2026 binder methods ship code-first on GitHub/Zenodo/company sites with no arXiv footprint — means our detective heuristic should watch Zenodo "binder" deposits, Tamarind's tool list, Claude-skill registries, Proteinbase, and the Adaptyv Substack, not arXiv listings. The single highest-leverage discovery is that the organizer has effectively open-sourced its scoring function: Adaptyv's `protein-qc` thresholds and the EGFR post-mortem together tell us to gate expression with SolubleMPNN first, rank by a composite in which ESM-C/ESM3 log-likelihood and interface ΔG matter more than the iPAE most pipelines filter on, and hedge de-novo risk by submitting diversified variants of anything that already binds.

What has changed going into the final three days is the order of operations. The generators (β-pairing RFdiffusion and Proteina-Complexa, both aimed at Region-I) and the conditional installer (Baker's pH-filter + HBNet) feed a pipeline whose *gate* is now expression and whose *judge* is a composite plus our own two-state physics — the reverse of the common de-novo instinct to optimize iPAE and check expression last. The refiner question, which started this report, resolves cleanly: we do not need ArcRefine's binary or its license, because we own its engine and can give it the one thing it lacks — an objective that knows about pH, species, and solubility all at once.
