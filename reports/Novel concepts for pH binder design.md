# Cooperative histidine networks sharpen our pH-switch

The single highest-value upgrade to our de-novo, pH-switchable, mouse-cross-reactive TNF-α mini-binder is to stop relying on one interface histidine and build a **cooperative multi-His switch whose pKa values are deliberately tuned into the 6.0–7.4 window**. Our current switch is modest (~2–20×) for a precise, quantitative reason: placing a single His next to the conserved target Lys both buries it (a measured ~2-unit pKa downshift) and flanks it with a like-charge cation, together suppressing its pKa toward **~4**, so it barely protonates even at pH 6.0 and almost no repulsion is gained. Three independent lines of evidence converge on the same fix: Baker's pH-binder paper (every working switch had **≥2 His–cation contacts**, scaling to **122–1000×**, [PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)); designed buried-His systems that switch over just **~0.3 pH units** using 6–9 coupled histidines ([Nat. Nanotechnol. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41565-024-01641-1)); and the thermodynamics of proton linkage (sharpness ∝ Δn_protons, [PMC9775224](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9775224)). The convergent remedy is to **couple ≥2 histidines (Hill n>1), raise each pKa with binder-side (intramolecular) electrostatics rather than a target bridge, and where possible move the switch into a buried core network (Baker's "Mechanism 2")**, which both got ~79× on a TNF-α binder and preserves the species-variable epitope for mouse cross-reactivity. Separately, the flat-polar affinity wall is best attacked by targeting the conserved **Region-I hydrophobic cluster (Trp114/Tyr115/Val74/Leu75/Ile97)** that yielded sub-nM de-novo minibinders (PDB 9JEC, [PMC12644665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12644665)), using an all-atom generator (RFdiffusion3) on a discontinuous epitope-only template with a hydrophilic-occupancy MPNN penalty. In three days on 8×H100, the realistic win is a portfolio that is cooperative, pKa-tuned, core-switched, and ranked by AF2-initial-guess plus explicit two-state physics — never by a learned protein–protein affinity score, which is unreliable in this regime ([arXiv 2512.06592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06592)).

## Why one interface histidine barely protonates — and the three-way fix that converges

The mechanistic core of this report is a number. A single titratable site obeys Henderson–Hasselbalch with Hill coefficient **n=1**, so its cationic fraction is f = 1/(1+10^(pH−pKa)). Even a *perfectly placed* His with pKa at the window center (6.7) swings only from f(7.4)≈0.17 to f(6.0)≈0.83 across the 1.4-unit window — a ceiling of ~0.66 in protonation change, and this is the best case ([coupled-protonation review, PMC2144168](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2144168)). Our His is far from that best case. Two effects *both* push its pKa down: **burial at the interface desolvates the charge (a measured ~2-unit downshift for solvent-excluded sidechains)** and the **adjacent conserved Lys⁺ destabilizes the protonated His⁺ by like-charge repulsion** ([JustHISpKa, PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)). The result is a His pinned near **pKa ~4**, for which f(7.4)≈0.0004 and f(6.0)≈0.01 — the imidazole is essentially neutral across the *entire* operating window, so protonation delivers almost no repulsion and the switch stays in the observed 2–20× regime. This is the "pKa-suppression trap," and it explains the modest switch exactly.

Three fixes converge, and the strongest design uses all three rather than choosing among them. **First, cooperativity.** Coupling ≥2 histidines turns n=1 into n>1: the protonation of one His raises the effective protonation of its neighbors, compressing the transition and summing the proton uptake so that |Δn_H|>1, which by Wyman linkage multiplies the switching free energy ΔΔG_pH = RT·ln10·∫Δn_H(pH)·dpH ([PMC9775224](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9775224); [Wyman & Gill, *Binding and Linkage*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/816655/binding-and-linkage-by-jeffries-wyman-stanley-gill)). This is not hypothetical: Baker's working interface switches *all* carried ≥2 protonated-His↔cation contacts, random His incorporation failed, and mutating either the His or its partner Lys abolished pH dependence ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)); designed buried-His filaments with 6 or 9 coupled His switch over just **~0.3 pH units** ([Nat. Nanotechnol. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41565-024-01641-1); [Boyken et al., Science 2019, PMC7072037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072037)).

**Second, re-raise each His pKa into 6.0–7.4 — and do it from the binder side.** The suppression is the enemy, so the fix is to add a **second-shell carboxylate (Asp/Glu) or backbone-carbonyl H-bond acceptor on the binder** positioned to stabilize His⁺, leaving the His partially solvent-exposed, and to confirm with JustHISpKa (already in the repo) that each switch His lands at pKa ~6.7–7.2 — the documented success window from the anti-Her2 study, where working switches had predicted pKa ~5.9–6.6 and failures were fully buried His with pKa<4 ([PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)). The non-obvious design rule, rarely articulated, is that the pKa-raising stabilizer **must be intramolecular**: a His⁺···*target*-carboxylate bridge would *add* attraction at low pH and make binding constitutive — consistent with our own finding that a buried His···carboxylate OFF-state (~−12 kcal/mol) binds both states. Tune the pKa with binder-side electrostatics; keep the repulsion on the target side.

**Third, move the switch into the core (Baker's M2) for the flat polar target and for mouse cross-reactivity.** A buried His-His/His-Lys charged H-bond network in the binder core destabilizes the *fold* on protonation and raises the off-rate globally, which is **target-agnostic** (ideal for a flat polar β-jellyroll that presents no tidy exposed cation cluster) and, critically, lives away from the species-variable epitope so it **preserves cross-species reactivity** — Baker's paper frames M2 as the affinity- and cross-species-preserving method and got **~79× on a de-novo TNF-α binder** with it ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)). For our mouse requirement this is decisive: the TNFR1-blocking determinants (including the dual-receptor-critical **Tyr87** and the 143–147 region) are conserved enough that human TNF binds mouse p55, whereas the TNFR2-leaning positions (Arg31/Arg32, 86, 146) are species-variable ([PMC12644665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12644665); human-vs-mouse ~70–77% identity, [R&D Systems](https://resources.rndsystems.com/pdfs/datasheets/fab410v.pdf)). A core switch keeps the cross-reactive contacts untouched. The engineering synthesis is therefore a single sentence: build a **cooperative, pKa-tuned His network, sited in the binder core where possible and backed by a ≥2-His interface cluster against the conserved K90/K98**, and score its sharpness as a proton-fluctuation object.

## Abstract mathematics: cooperative-titration partition functions, surface descriptors, and a novelty frontier

The mathematics that matters most here is the **statistical mechanics of coupled protonation**. An Ising model with a handful of intrinsic pKas plus a short-ranged pairwise coupling matrix W_ij quantitatively reproduces multi-site titration, and the Hill coefficient — or, more sensitively, the proton capacitance dn̄/dpH — is the scalar that measures cooperativity ([PMC2144168](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2144168); [pepKalc](https://st-protein02.chem.au.dk/pepkalc/)). Coupling strength is a *geometric* design knob: the cooperativity of poly-ionizable systems depends strongly on the distance between ionizable groups, demonstrated for hexaamines ([RSC a907397c](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/1999/cp/a907397c)). This yields a crisp, cheap objective that is **new to this round as a selection filter**: for each candidate His/acid cluster, enumerate the 2^N microstates, populate W_ij and intrinsic pKas from GFN2-xTB capped-cluster energies or PROPKA, build the partition function Z(pH), and accept designs with n>1 and large ∫Δn_H — the optimization target being degenerate His pKas and large positive couplings. Bifurcation theory sharpens the intuition: increasing n_H moves a graded response toward a bistable toggle via a saddle-node, so the diagnostic to run on a design's ΔG_bind(pH) curve is whether pH-6 protonation *annihilates* the bound basin rather than merely shallowing it ([toggle-switch bifurcations, arXiv:2509.24926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24926); [buffering near criticality, arXiv:2212.08600](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08600)). No published work applies catastrophe theory to protonation-driven binder release, so treat this as a diagnostic, not a generator.

For the **flat-polar geometry problem**, the leverage is in rotation-invariant surface descriptors — a genuinely new angle versus the prior dossier's topological/optimal-transport framing. **3D Zernike descriptors** expand a solvent-excluded surface patch into a rotation-invariant coefficient vector that can carry *both* shape and electrostatic potential; the LZerD/PatchSurfer family finds near-native poses in the top ranks in 74% of bound cases, and a 2025 benchmark retrieves surfaces by shape+ESP jointly ([3DZD, BMC Bioinformatics](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1471-2105-10-407); [PatchSurfer](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12859-018-2043-3); [SHREC 2025, arXiv:2509.12976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12976)). **MaSIF** treats the surface as a manifold and learns interaction fingerprints by geodesic convolution over curvature + Poisson–Boltzmann electrostatics + H-bond features, exposing exactly the polar/geometric determinants that contact a partner ([MaSIF, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/606202.full.pdf); [EPFL](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/273279)). Both can be run as cheap post-hoc filters: compute 3DZD on 1TNF epitope sub-patches to rank the most-designable compact sub-site, and on each design's interface to score shape+ESP complementarity, as a pose-invariant complement to AF2-ig (which our own data show can be fooled by self-sequence refolds). Optimal transport via fused Gromov–Wasserstein gives the same complementarity as an alignment-free cost between polar-group point clouds ([OTMol, arXiv:2509.01550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01550)).

Two further frameworks reframe old problems. **Multistate design with explicit negative design** is the mature, low-risk formalization of our two-state requirement: it chooses a sequence *against* undesired states, has redesigned homodimers into obligate heterodimers, and reportedly beats single-state design by ~15% on a conformational benchmark ([Rosetta MSF, PMC5484525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5484525); [ProteinInterfaceMSMover](https://docs.rosettacommons.org/docs/latest/scripting_documentation/RosettaScripts/Movers/movers_pages/ProteinInterfaceMSMover)). Operationally this is nearly free — run the oracle twice (pH-7.4 vs pH-6.0 protonation) and require PASS@7.4 ∧ FAIL@6.0, which directly fixes the "constitutive bridge passes direction but not selectivity" failure. **Spectral graph theory** supplies a cooperativity test: build the MD-weighted interface contact graph and require the switch His to sit on a high-edge-betweenness node whose protonation projects onto a *low-frequency, global* interface mode (breaks many contacts at once) rather than a local one ([perturbation-network analysis, PMC6604606](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6604606); [structure-network review, PMC7395124](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7395124)). Finally, the **rate-distortion frontier** organizes the novelty-versus-binding tension that our memory flags ("sharpness-via-stacking fights novelty"): plot oracle score against a novelty coordinate (1−TM, or a TDA bottleneck distance) and select points *on* the empirical frontier, with element-specific persistent homology as the affinity-correlated feature and persistent-H2 interface voids penalized as packing defects ([ESPH, arXiv:1703.10982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10982); [persistent directed-flag Laplacian, arXiv:2411.02596](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02596v1); [rate-distortion molecular codes, arXiv:1007.4471](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.4471)). Tropical geometry and category theory surfaced as the speculative tail with **no protein-design precedent** — park them.

## Abstract physics: from 0.3-pH-unit cooperative switches to interfacial-water engineering

The physics this round pushes hardest is the one with the best empirical ceiling: **cooperative two-state protonation as an Ising machine**. De-novo buried-His hydrogen-bond networks already produce cooperative, large-scale conformational change on acidification, with transition pH and steepness tunable by the number of His networks versus surrounding hydrophobic packing, and the sharpest designs switch over ~0.3 pH units ([Boyken, PMC7072037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072037); [Nat. Nanotechnol. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41565-024-01641-1); [Janelia summary](https://www.janelia.org/publication/de-novo-design-of-tunable-ph-driven-conformational-changes)). The 1D-Ising mapping is exact — conformational energy is a sum of intrinsic unit stabilities plus neighbor couplings, ligand saturation maps to magnetization and log-concentration (here, pH) to the external field — and cooperative transitions are sharp precisely when the heterogeneity of stabilizing interactions is *small* (the random-field Ising result), which tells us to make the His pKas degenerate and the couplings uniform ([Ising repeat-protein review, par.nsf.gov 10223765](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10223765); [RFIM folding, cond-mat/9606136](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9606136)). The highest-leverage move is to port this proven core-destabilization machine to *interface* destabilization: a 2–4-His zipper that is a neutral H-bond staple at pH 7.4 and injects +2/+3 into a low-dielectric interface patch at pH 6.0.

Three coupling tiers make this computable within budget, and ECC is the correction that keeps it honest. **Tier 1 (screen):** build the site-site interaction matrix W_ij from an APBS Poisson–Boltzmann solve at fixed geometry and Ising-fit to get n_H across thousands of RFd3 backbones before folding. **Tier 2 (rank):** alchemical double-free-energy ddG gives coupled pKas with quantitative pairwise coupling, cutting pKa error roughly in half versus coupling-blind estimates ([Wilson et al., JCC 2024](https://www.mpinat.mpg.de/4706156/Wilson_2024_JCC.pdf)). **Tier 3 (confirm):** continuous constant-pH (λ-dynamics) MD gives the actual titration curve Y(pH) to fit n_H ([Donnini et al., JCTC 2016](https://www.em.mpg.de/634247/Donnini_2016_JCTC.pdf); [continuous cpHMD, PMC3752133](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3752133)). Because fixed-charge Amber over-counts both the His⁺···Lys⁺ repulsion and any compensating salt bridge, apply the **electronic-continuum correction — scale charged-group charges by ~0.75 (1/√ε∞)** — or use GFN2-xTB, and adopt a switch only if its sign and ordering survive both ([ECC review, arXiv:2004.07702](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2004.07702); [ECC ion pairing, arXiv:2308.05482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05482)). This is the physics behind our memory lesson that fixed-charge MD occupancy is chemistry-biased.

The genuinely novel physics bets fall into two tiers. The best new lever is **molecular-titration ("proton-decoy") ultrasensitivity**: a cluster of coupled off-interface buffer His gives an all-or-none threshold controlled by B_T/K_d, and non-allosteric ultrasensitivity mechanisms reach effective Hill coefficients of n~10–32 — far beyond the n~2.8 of hemoglobin — so a buffer relay could sharpen the transition without crowding the interface ([Ultrasensitivity, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasensitivity); [zero-order as criticality, IIT Madras](https://publications.iitm.ac.in/pdf/preprint-zero-order-ultrasensitivity-a-study-of-criticality)). **Electrostatic frustration** turns the frustration index into a switch objective — reward designs whose interface is minimally frustrated (every charge satisfied) at pH 7.4 and maximally frustrated (mutually unsatisfiable like-charges) at pH 6.0 ([Ferreiro et al., PNAS 2007](https://nfkb-ikb.ucsd.edu/Coll_Pubs/Ferreiro_2007b.pdf); [spin-glass optimal frustration, cond-mat/0109044](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0109044)). For the flat-polar affinity problem specifically, the physics says van der Waals packing is the wrong lever and **interfacial-water thermodynamics and long-range electrostatics are the right ones**. Three concrete moves follow: **electrostatic steering** (established but underused) can accelerate association up to ~50× and makes a shallow-well interface competitive by funneling k_on, and the steering field is itself pH-tunable so k_on can be designed to drop at pH 6.0 ([steering kinetics, Univ. Kentucky](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/influence-of-electrostatic-forces-on-the-association-kinetics-and/); [Langevin reaction patches, arXiv:0809.2871](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.2871)); **hot-water displacement** (WaterMap-style) maps H-bond-frustrated "hot" waters on apo-TNF that release favorably when displaced, a target-only explicit-solvent precompute done once for both orthologs; and **controlled local dewetting** exploits the fact that protein hydration sits at the edge of a drying transition, so a small apolar binder patch can trigger an entropy-driven attraction that is also pH-tippable ([hydration near dewetting, PMC2861827](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861827); [confined-water evaporation, Berne group](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/chemistry/groups/berne/papers/jpcC_113_5244_2009.pdf)). The glamour-but-risky far tier — **critical-Casimir near-dewetting amplification** ([Machta et al., arXiv:1203.2199](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1203.2199)), **mechanical/metamaterial allosteric-lever off-switches** that decouple the proton sensor from the contact residues ([Rocks et al., PNAS 2017, arXiv:1607.08562](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.08562); [Flechsig, arXiv:1702.08317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.08317)), and **nuclear-quantum/low-barrier-H-bond flip-point tuning** ([NQE in proton transfer, PMC10801683](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10801683/)) — should be touched only if the core campaign converges early; the mechanical-lever route in particular fights our prior "hinged-lid unbuildable" lesson. **PCET and non-equilibrium/stochastic-thermodynamic sharpening are honest no-gos** for a passive equilibrium binder: they need a redox partner or a continuously driven energy source that a fixed-pH assay does not provide ([Tyr PCET, RSC d3sc05450k](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/sc/d3sc05450k)).

## Abstract chemistry: escape the pKa-suppression trap with binder-side electrostatics

The chemistry note is the newest material this round and its headline is the design rule already stated: **tune the switch-His pKa into 6.7–7.2 with an intramolecular, binder-side carboxylate or backbone-carbonyl acceptor, while keeping the like-charge repulsion partner on the target**. Histidine is uniquely suited because its solvent-exposed pKa (~6.5) is the only sidechain near neutral pH and its microenvironment can shift it across a span of ~2–9, with JustHISpKa predicting it to MUE 0.40 / RMSE 0.52 pH units and flagging His coupled within 6 Å — exactly the coupling we want to engineer ([PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)). The enabling primitive for both the interface zipper and the core network is **designed hydrogen-bond networks (HBNet/MC-HBNet)**, which search sequence+rotamer space for self-contained "closed" networks where every buried polar group is satisfied ([HBNet, PMC5497568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5497568); [MC-HBNet](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/42/89d580f79a3831ed81abefcd08d55d56/rapid-sampling-of-hydrogen-bond-networks-for-computational-protein-design.pdf)). Buried salt bridges are proven fold-coupled pH sensors — an Asp302–His305 pair halves unfolding ΔG as His deprotonates, and García-Moreno's "insert a charge in the hydrophobic interior" paradigm is patented explicitly as a pH-switch strategy ([buried salt-bridge review, PMC3086997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3086997); [US Patent 10,138,471](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10138471)).

Beyond His, three chemistries widen the design space. A **carboxyl-carboxylate dyad** (two Asp/Glu ~2.3–3 Å apart sharing a proton) can split pKas by up to ~6 units, pushing one carboxyl above 8, which gives access to the pH-6.0 edge of the window where His is already fading; the direction for a PPI on/off switch is unproven, so it is a NOVEL-TO-DESIGN bet to validate by PROPKA/pKAI sign-consensus plus xTB proton-shared-vs-doubly-ionized energies ([paired carboxylic acids, ACS figshare](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/Paired_Carboxylic_Acids_in_Enzymes_and_Their_Role_in_Selective_Substrate_Binding_Catalysis_and_Unusually_Shifted_p_i_K_i_sub_a_sub_Values/8976902); [Li et al. 2004](https://ns9999k.webs.sigma2.no/10.11582_2021.00081/2016_Tiwari_Reuter_TIMs_PlosCB/tim%20barrel%20pdfs/Li-2004-The%20determinants%20of%20carboxyl%20pKa%20value.pdf)). A **His-based cation-π contact** doubles as both a flat-surface affinity hotspot (~3 kcal/mol each, present in roughly half of complexes) and a switch, if the aromatic is positioned so that His⁺ is expelled at low pH ([cation-π in PPIs, Univ. Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/cation-%CF%80-interactions-in-protein-protein-interfaces/)). **Metal-coordination** (hexa-His sites chelating a divalent metal that is ejected on protonation) and **non-canonical Dap** (β-amine pKa ~6.3, driving pH 5↔7 conformational change) are SPECULATIVE because they need a cofactor or break the 20-canonical constraint ([TriCyt, PMC7983065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7983065); [Dap vectors, hal-02179234](https://hal-univ-evry.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02179234)).

On the affinity side, the chemistry that attacks the flat polar surface most directly and is tool-native is **β-strand augmentation / edge-strand invasion**. RFdiffusion can be conditioned to grow an extended β-sheet that matches a target edge β-strand, turning the target's own polar backbone into the binding determinant — backbone-backbone H-bonds are shape-insensitive and pH-robust — and this was built explicitly to improve binders to *polar* surfaces, reaching pM–mid-nM with crystallographic confirmation on KIT, PDGFRα, and others ([β-pairing RFdiffusion, bioRxiv 2024.10.11.617496](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.11.617496.full.pdf); [Nat. Commun. s41467-025-67866-3](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/365/4c42c439d6300d56d83cbc25f1b2b04d/s41467-025-67866-3_reference.pdf)). A TNF-α β-jellyroll exposes edge strands, making this the most actionable Q2 idea — it is an RFdiffusion mode, not a new tool. Backed by **designed water-mediated H-bond networks** (which improve polar-interface prediction and reportedly reach ≥300× specificity, [Frontiers Mol. Biosci. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2018.00088/pdf); [Baker-lab water design](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/jiang05A.pdf)) and the 2–3 cation-π pairs above, these can supply the missing hydrophobic-hotspot energy on a surface that offers few anchors. **Halogen bonds** (σ-hole contacts rivaling H-bonds) are a non-canonical reach with no native tool support — mention only, do not pursue ([XB thesis, MountainScholar](https://mountainscholar.org/items/90786c38-b68d-4152-a996-2567c902e7b0); [protein XB structure-energy, NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10453085)).

## Latest methods: all-atom generators and discontinuous epitopes crack the flat-polar wall

The 2024–2026 generator frontier has split into AF2-hallucination pipelines and all-atom diffusion/flow co-design, and for a flat polar cytokine the **all-atom generators are the right class** because they place explicit side chains and rotamers at the interface rather than deferring everything to a downstream MPNN. **RFdiffusion3** diffuses backbone *and* side-chain atoms (the atom, not the residue, is the diffused unit), explicitly models ligands and metals, runs ~10× faster than RFdiffusion2, and is open via Rosetta Commons Foundry (Dec 2025) — and our own memory already records RFd3 breaking the TNF-α wall to af2ig PAE 13.0 ([RFd3, bioRxiv 2025.09.18.676967](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.18.676967); [IPD](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/12/rfdiffusion3-now-available/)). **BoltzGen** (MIT-licensed, already in-stack) unifies folding and design across nanobodies, miniproteins, and stapled peptides, was validated across 26 targets, and — directly relevant — includes **TNF-α in a 5-target benchmark at 80% target-level success**, with design-spec levers (negative "not-binding" residue annotations, secondary-structure conditioning) that let us steer *away* from the polar subunit-interface patch and toward an edge strand ([BoltzGen, PMC12697729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12697729); [GitHub](https://github.com/HannesStark/boltzgen)). The two highest-hit-rate generators, **AlphaProteo** (9–88% across seven targets) and **Chai-2** (68% miniprotein success, routinely picomolar), are proprietary and effectively unavailable ([AlphaProteo, arXiv 2409.08022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08022); [Chai-2, bioRxiv 2025.07.05.663018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.05.663018)). The decisive lesson is the **TNF-α contradiction**: AlphaProteo *explicitly failed on TNF-α*, attributing it to the "flat, highly polar" subunit-interface patch, yet classical Rosetta and BoltzGen succeed on the hydrophobic Region-I cleft — so the "unbindable" label attaches to the *epitope*, not the molecule ([arXiv 2409.08022 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08022); [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/open-problems/de-novo-binders-to-tnfa)).

Four hard-target best-practices should shape the campaign. **Pick a compact, contactable, ideally hydrophobic epitope** — for TNF-α that is Region-I (Val74/Leu75/Ile97 on one monomer + Trp114/Tyr115 on the adjacent), the choice behind the sub-nM minibinders. **Shrink the target to a discontinuous epitope-only template**: EpitopeCraft retains only the surface residues around the binding site and exploits folding NNs' "local-first" bias, delivering up to **80% in-silico success improvement** on difficult targets and up to **44× faster** per success, with a Monte-Carlo evolution step giving +130–364% and a **hydrophilic-occupancy MPNN penalty** (penalizing unpaired polar interface residues) plus a surface-charge penalty as companion moves ([EpitopeCraft, arXiv 2509.25479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25479)). **Partial-diffuse validated binders** rather than pure de-novo (our data: ~11% pass vs ~0.3%). And **engage β-strand conformations** — RFdiffusion already designs β-strand binders to IDR/edge targets at Kd 3–100 nM ([Nature s41586-025-09248-9](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/s41586-025-09248-9-compressed.pdf)). On ranking, the field consensus is firm and bears directly on our selection: **AF2-initial-guess pae_interaction/ipTM is the validated primary signal**, confirmed to outperform Rosetta energies across a meta-analysis of 3,766 experimentally characterized binders, while **Boltz-2's affinity head does not transfer to protein–protein** — a Dec 2025 study shows a fine-tuned Boltz-2-PPI underperforms sequence-based models despite high structural accuracy ([meta-analysis, bioRxiv 2025.08.14.670059](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.14.670059); [Boltz-2-PPI, arXiv 2512.06592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06592); [AF2-IG, Nat. Commun. 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v14y2023i1d10.1038_s41467-023-38328-5.html)). Rank de-novo TNF-α binders by AF2-IG plus physics rescoring (Rosetta ddG/SC/SAP, MM-GBSA); treat any learned PP-affinity number as low-trust. Other open options worth knowing: **BindCraft** (10–100% success, [PMC11642741](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11642741)), **EvoBind2** (sequence-only cyclic/linear peptides, 46–75%, [PMC12280060](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12280060)), **Germinal** (epitope-targeted nanobodies, 4–22%, [bioRxiv 2025.09.19.677421](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421)), **ProteinGenerator** (sequence-space diffusion with demonstrated multistate design, [bioRxiv 2023.05.08.539766](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.05.08.539766)), **La-Proteina** ([arXiv 2507.09466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09466)), and a **TNFR-superfamily target-conditioned diffusion** paper directly in-family ([bioRxiv 2024.09.13.612773](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.13.612773)).

## Latest pH-switch and TNF papers: Baker's blueprint, Chugai's antibodies, and the conserved epitope

The **Baker/IPD pH-sensitive binders paper** (Ahn, Coventry, …, Baker; bioRxiv 2025-09-29) is the direct blueprint and already includes a TNF-α and a TNFR2 switch ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/); [DOI 10.1101/2025.09.29.678932](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.09.29.678932)). Its two mechanisms map exactly onto our fixes: Mechanism 1 places interface His adjacent to target Arg/Lys/His (protonation → repulsion; all working switches had ≥2 contacts at 3.7–7.6 Å), and Mechanism 2 buries a His-containing charged H-bond network in the binder core (protonation destabilizes the fold, "preserves binding affinity and cross-species reactivity"). Fold-changes at pH 7.4→5.4 reached up to 1000× (EphA2, interface), 122× (TNFR2, interface), ~79× (a de-novo TNF-α binder, network), down to 6× (IL-6) and 3.5× (PCSK9). The pipeline is diffusion/MPNN/AF2/Rosetta-native: RFdiffusion backbones → ProteinMPNN with His-bias (0.5/1.0/1.5) and *donor/acceptor specification* (naive His enrichment gave "little pH-sensitivity") → AF2 filter (pae_interaction<8, pLDDT>88) → partial diffusion + FastRelax → selection on a burial-weighted "pH score," a fa_elec-only ΔΔG_elec≥0 filter, and His↔cation rotamer distances. Crucially, **no pKa predictor is in their loop** — they rely on the intrinsic His pKa ~6.5 — which is precisely the gap our JustHISpKa targeting fills, because the pKa-suppression trap means the *effective* in-complex pKa is what decides the switch, not the textbook value.

The antibody field established the release-at-low-pH paradigm a decade earlier and adds levers de-novo designers under-use. Chugai's recycling antibodies use **His scanning** in CDRs to release antigen in the acidified endosome, extended to "sweeping" antibodies via FcRn/pI engineering ([Igawa et al., Nat. Biotechnol. 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/nbt.1691)). Because broad His scanning limits epitopes and gives incomplete release, Chugai also invented **Ca²⁺-dependent binding** (release driven by low endosomal [Ca²⁺], PDB 5AZE) — an orthogonal, epitope-agnostic trigger worth holding as a fallback if a His switch stays too shallow ([RCSB 5AZE](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/5AZE)). **SIpHAB** is a sequence-only predictor of His mutations for pH-biased binding, trainable *against* acidic pH (our direction), usable as a cheap triage filter without a complex structure ([DOI 10.1080/19420862.2024.2404064](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2024.2404064)). And a 2021 stepwise His→acidic-residue mutagenesis reached ~32× pH-selective binding best in the **pH 5.5–6.0 range**, confirming that pairing His with engineered Asp/Glu sharpens the acidic edge of our window ([DOI 10.1007/s13205-021-03079-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13205-021-03079-x)). The broader de-novo switch toolkit — LOCKR/Co-LOCKR Boolean logic gates and 2024 allosterically switchable assemblies with **hemoglobin-level cooperativity from global energetic coupling** — reinforces the thesis that steep switches come from coupling many small energetics, not one contact ([Co-LOCKR](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Lajoie-coLOCKR2020.pdf); [de-novo MWC allostery, Nature 2024](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/323/93bad0a8e360a61c7ad5034bc5291fb8/s41586-024-07813-2.pdf)).

The TNF-α biology sets the hard constraints for cross-reactivity. The receptor-binding site sits in the **cleft between two adjacent trimer subunits**; hot-spots cluster in three loops (~29–34, ~84–89, ~143–147) with **Tyr87 critical for both receptors** ([TNF–TNFR2, PDB 3ALQ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/3ALQ)). The subtype split is the key: the 29–34 loop plus 86 and 146 preferentially drive the species-variable **TNFR2 (p75)**, while 143–145 drive the conserved **TNFR1 (p55)** — and human TNF binds mouse p55 but not mouse p75, so the TNFR1-blocking surface is the conserved, cross-reactive one. Clinical mAbs (adalimumab PDB 3WD5, infliximab) bind conformational epitopes and **do not cross-react with murine TNF**, so our conserved, receptor-blocking epitope must exploit the TNFR1 determinants, not the TNFR2 region ([3WD5](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/3WD5)). This is exactly why the core-network switch (Mechanism 2) is strategically superior for our spec: it keeps the switch off the species-variable interface. The one closed gap is that no 2024–2026 paper combines TNF-α + de-novo + pH-switch + *mouse* cross-reactivity in one; the Baker paper (TNF-α pH-switch) and the Comm. Biol. human/canine minibinders (PDB 9JEC, no pH) are the two closest, non-overlapping precedents, and the exact mouse-vs-human identity at the switch anchors K90/K98 must be confirmed by alignment before committing the switch chemistry.

## Calibration and caveats: the 6.0 window is hard and our tools are partly blind

Four calibrations keep the plan honest. **First, the 7.4→6.0 window is intrinsically harder than Baker's headline 7.4→5.4 demonstrations.** His pKa sits near 6.0, so by pH 6.0 only about half the imidazole is protonated even without suppression; the *only* Baker example measured at pH 6.0 (the Neo2 core-network binder) achieved just **>2×**, versus 79–1000× at pH 5.4. Expect raw fold-changes smaller than the headline numbers, and prioritize cooperativity precisely because the window is narrow ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)). **Second, there is no published Hill coefficient for a PPI pH-switch** — Baker reports a cooperative unfolding transition only for a 9-H-bond homo-oligomer control, not for the binders, and the antibody literature reports fold-changes, not cooperativity. The achievable n_H on a rigid two-protein interface (versus an IDP or an assembly) is genuinely unknown and must be measured in our own Ising/constant-pH-MD ladder, not assumed. **Third, rank de-novo binders by AF2-IG, not Boltz PP-affinity**: the Boltz-2 affinity head is a real advance for small molecules but does not transfer to protein–protein, and for the pH-switch *magnitude* no learned model is reliable — explicit two-state physics (MD occupancy delta, xTB ΔE_switch, PROPKA/Wyman linkage on the exact submitted sequence) remains the honest metric ([arXiv 2512.06592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06592)). **Fourth, our default MD is partly blind to the physics that dominates a flat polar interface**: Amber + GBn2 implicit solvent cannot see interfacial-water thermodynamics, dewetting, or ordered bridging waters, so every water-based affinity lever (hot-water displacement, controlled dewetting, water networks) needs a separate *explicit-solvent* validation track and cannot be screened on GBn2 alone.

Three tool-accuracy caveats bound the switch-design loop. JustHISpKa's MUE 0.40 was measured on *natural* proteins; whether it holds for a de-novo interface His in a bound complex is unverified, and buried-charge pKas are notoriously predictor-divergent (our own memory: ≥2 methods disagree 2–3 units on buried carboxylates), which is why a buried acid flip-point is undesignable and His — with its natural in-window pKa — is the right sensor. GFN2-xTB microstate pKa (SAMPL6 MAE ~1.74 pKa units) is too coarse to resolve a midpoint to the ±0.3 units this window demands, so it serves as a consensus vote and a fair *relative* ΔE_switch referee, never a sole oracle ([k-dense SAMPL6](https://www.k-dense.ai/use-cases/sampl6-first-principles-pka-prediction)). And MSM/large-deviation k_off — the most physically faithful selectivity readout — is sampling-limited: converging a de-novo mini-binder's dissociation on a protein–protein interface within three days is uncertain, so any kinetic number (including infrequent-metadynamics koff from the prior dossier) is a *relative* 7.4-vs-6.0 ranking filter, not an absolute rate. The irreducible risk is biological, not computational: whether a sharp charged-anchor switch and genuine mouse cross-reactivity can coexist is decided by *where the conserved epitope sits relative to the switch anchor*, which is why the K90/K98 conservation check is the first thing to run and the one result that can redirect the campaign.

## Conclusion

The new synthesis this round is a mechanism, not a menu: the modest switch is a *quantitative* consequence of His pKa suppressed toward ~4 by burial plus an adjacent cation, and the three fixes the literature offers — cooperative ≥2-His coupling for Hill n>1, binder-side pKa re-tuning into 6.7–7.2, and relocation into a buried core network — are not competing options but facets of one design, because the core network delivers cooperativity *and* pKa control *and* cross-reactivity preservation simultaneously. That reframes the campaign's hardest trade-off, mouse cross-reactivity versus a sharp charged-anchor switch, from a collision into a complementarity: put the affinity on the conserved Region-I/TNFR1 contacts and the switch in the core, and the two objectives stop fighting. The second durable insight is a measurement economy — cooperative sharpness, pH selectivity, and cross-reactivity sensitivity are the same proton-fluctuation object, so one constant-pH ensemble per design scores all three, which is exactly the physics the Ahn baseline never computed. The boldest differentiators (molecular-titration ultrasensitivity, electrostatic-frustration flips, interfacial-water engineering) remain empty of de-novo PPI precedent, so they are where the ceiling is highest and the evidence thinnest; they earn GPU only after the cooperative-pKa-tuned-core-network core has converged. For three days on 8×H100, the disciplined bet is to spend compute making the switch *cooperative and correctly protonated* and the affinity *conserved and Region-I-anchored*, and to rank the result by AF2-IG plus explicit two-state physics — not to chase a glamorous mechanism that the window, the tools, and the biology are not yet ready to reward.
