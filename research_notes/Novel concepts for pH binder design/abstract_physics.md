# Abstract-Physics Frameworks for a Drastically Better de-novo pH-Switchable TNF-alpha Binder

Scope note for the report writer: this catalog targets a de-novo mini-protein binder (20 natural AAs) to human TNF-alpha (flat, polar, antibody-only beta-jellyroll cytokine, PDB 1TNF). Priorities: (1) a SHARP, STRONG pH-SELECTIVE off-switch (bind pH 7.4, RELEASE at pH 6.0 via protonation BREAKING binding); (2) mouse cross-reactivity; (3) affinity on a flat polar surface. Current switch = a single interface His next to a conserved target Lys (His+ repels Lys+ at low pH), which is MODEST (~2-20x). Toolchain assumed throughout: RFdiffusion3 (all-atom), ProteinMPNN, AF2-initial-guess + Boltz-2 (binding oracles), OpenMM MD (Amber ff + GBn2 implicit solvent), GFN2-xTB QM, PROPKA/pKa predictors. Every entry is tagged ESTABLISHED vs SPECULATIVE and given a concrete in-silico test. Project lessons honored: fixed-charge MD occupancy is chemistry-biased (use xTB for fair switch energetics); selectivity needs explicit-state MD occupancy deltas; confirm buried-pKa flip-points with >=2 pKa methods; score pH-selectivity as a Wyman linkage integral. A sibling note (`Novel abstract concepts for TNF binders/abstract_physics.md`) already covers charge-regulation capacitance and buried-His networks in depth; this note minimizes overlap and pushes the exotic/rarely-used physics harder.

## Q1: Which physics can SHARPEN the pH transition and STRENGTHEN the low-pH release?

### Takeaway
The transition sharpness is a cooperativity (effective Hill n_H) problem and the release strength is a free-energy-sign problem; both are addressable with physics far beyond "one His next to one Lys." The highest-leverage established lever is a cooperative multi-His two-state partition function (Ising) with burial-amplified coupling (proven to give near-step transitions: designed pH filaments switch over 0.3 pH units). The highest-novelty levers are molecular-titration (stoichiometric proton-decoy) ultrasensitivity, engineered electrostatic frustration at the interface, and a mechanical/allosteric-network conformational off-switch. Nuclear-quantum, PCET, and non-equilibrium ideas are mostly speculative or out-of-scope for a passive equilibrium binder and are flagged as such.

### Cited Findings

**Two-state / Ising statistical mechanics of cooperative protonation (ESTABLISHED, directly portable).**
- De-novo buried His hydrogen-bond networks produce cooperative, large-scale conformational change on lowering pH; transition pH and cooperativity are tunable by the NUMBER of His networks vs the strength of surrounding hydrophobic packing — [Boyken et al., Science 2019, PMC7072037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072037); [Janelia summary](https://www.janelia.org/publication/de-novo-design-of-tunable-ph-driven-conformational-changes).
- De-novo pH-responsive filaments built from subunits with SIX or NINE buried His show a sharp, reversible assembled->disassembled transition over just ~0.3 pH units with disassembly in <1 s after a pH drop — [Nat. Nanotechnol. 2024, s41565-024-01641-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41565-024-01641-1).
- 1D-Ising "nearest-neighbor" models are the standard statistical-thermodynamic treatment of protein cooperativity; conformational energy is a sum of intrinsic unit stabilities plus coupling energies between neighbors, and the coupling magnitude sets system-wide cooperativity. In the ligand mapping, Ising magnetization corresponds to bound-ligand saturation Y and the external field h to log-concentration of ligand (here, to pH) — [Ising repeat-protein review, par.nsf.gov 10223765](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10223765); [Ferromagnetic models for cooperative behavior, arXiv:1307.4984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.4984).
- Cooperative two-state folding maps to the Random-Field Ising Model; cooperative (sharp) transitions occur when the heterogeneity of stabilizing interactions is SMALL — [Cooperativity of Protein Folding and the RFIM, cond-mat/9606136](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9606136).

**Ultrasensitivity mechanisms that are NOT classical cooperativity (ESTABLISHED in signaling, RARELY used in binder design).**
- Eight distinct mechanisms give Hill-like ultrasensitivity independent of allosteric cooperativity: multistep/multisite, buffering (molecular titration / stoichiometric decoys), localization change, zero-order saturation (Goldbeter-Koshland), positive feedback, allovalency, non-zero-order membrane, and dissipative allostery — [Ultrasensitivity, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasensitivity).
- Molecular-titration (stoichiometric buffering): a buffer B sequesters ligand A until B saturates, giving an all-or-none threshold near the equivalence point [A_T]~[B_T]; ultrasensitivity strength is set by the ratio B_T/K_d and strengthens as that ratio grows above 1 — [Ultrasensitivity, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasensitivity).
- Effective Hill coefficient n_H = log(81)/log(EC90/EC10); real systems reach very large n_H (Cdc25C n~32, bacterial flagellar motor n~10, MAPK cascade n~4-5 vs hemoglobin n~2.8), and cascade layers multiply: n <= n1*n2 — [Ultrasensitivity, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasensitivity).
- Zero-order ultrasensitivity is mathematically equivalent to allosteric cooperativity and its effective Hill coefficient can be made arbitrarily large near enzyme saturation; it has an explicit near-criticality interpretation — [zero-order ultrasensitivity as criticality, IIT Madras preprint](https://publications.iitm.ac.in/pdf/preprint-zero-order-ultrasensitivity-a-study-of-criticality).

**Electrostatic frustration & spin-glass theory (ESTABLISHED analysis tool; SPECULATIVE as a design driver).**
- Sites of high local frustration correlate with functional regions — binding sites and residues involved in allosteric transitions; the frustration framework now includes electrostatic energy terms — [Ferreiro et al., localized frustration, PNAS 2007](https://nfkb-ikb.ucsd.edu/Coll_Pubs/Ferreiro_2007b.pdf).
- Proteins behave optimally near the ferromagnet-to-spin-glass transition: "optimal frustration" is where the sequence entropy compatible with a single accessible ground state is maximal — [Proteinlike behavior near the ferromagnet/spin-glass transition, cond-mat/0109044](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0109044).
- Allostery can be evolved/designed as sparse, modular couplings in a network governed by a spin-glass-like energy function — [Evolution of sparsity and modularity in protein allostery, arXiv:1408.3240](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.3240).

**Critical phenomena / critical Casimir fluctuation forces (ESTABLISHED physics, HIGHLY SPECULATIVE here).**
- Near a critical point, order-parameter fluctuations produce a long-ranged Casimir-like force between inclusions: potential ~ d^(-1/4) for order-parameter coupling (d^(-2) for energy-density coupling), attractive between LIKE inclusions and repulsive between UNLIKE; the range equals the correlation length xi with decay ~ d^(-1/2) exp(-d/xi), and the force is longest-ranged exactly AT criticality — [Machta, Veatch, Sethna, critical Casimir forces in membranes, arXiv:1203.2199](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1203.2199); [popular summary](https://physicsworld.com/a/a-casimir-force-for-life/).
- Protein hydration shells sit at the EDGE of a dewetting (drying) transition, i.e., near-critical with respect to liquid-vapor coexistence of confined water — [hydration near dewetting transition, PMC2861827](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861827).

**Nuclear quantum effects / proton tunneling on pKa (ESTABLISHED in enzymology, SPECULATIVE for a designed switch).**
- Proton/H transfer can proceed by tunneling even where thermal barrier-crossing is expected; nuclear quantum effects (zero-point energy, tunneling) give kinetic isotope effects >100, and quantum delocalization can dramatically stabilize deprotonation, producing very large isotope effects on acidity (pKa) — [Nuclear Quantum Effects in Proton/H Transfer, PMC10801683](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10801683/).

**Proton-coupled electron transfer, PCET (ESTABLISHED in redox proteins; LIKELY OUT-OF-SCOPE here).**
- In redox-active Tyr, proton transfer is coupled to electron transfer because the phenol pKa shifts with redox state; de-novo scaffolds (alpha3Y / alpha3C) and non-canonical Tyr analogs let designers tune PCET mechanism by solvent exposure and internal H-bonding — [Tyrosine PCET in de-novo proteins, RSC Chem. Sci. 2024, d3sc05450k](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/sc/d3sc05450k).

**Mechanical-network / metamaterial allostery for a conformational off-switch (ESTABLISHED in metamaterials, NOVEL for binders).**
- Disordered elastic networks can be tuned to encode an arbitrary source->target strain response by pruning only ~1% of bonds; this reproduces allostery-like long-range mechanical coupling between remote sites — [Rocks et al., Designing allostery-inspired response in mechanical networks, PNAS 2017, arXiv:1607.08562](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.08562).
- Elastic networks with evolutionarily optimized long-range communication act as mechanical models of allosteric proteins; directional communication is governed by an "allosteric lever" that couples localized hard modes to concerted long-range soft-mode relaxation — [Flechsig, elastic-network allostery, arXiv:1702.08317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.08317).

### Inferences

- **[HIGH-VALUE, ESTABLISHED] Port the Boyken cooperative-His two-state machine from fold-switching to INTERFACE-switching.** Mechanism: place a cooperative cluster of 2-4 His so that at pH 7.4 they form a buried neutral H-bond zipper that staples the binder to TNF, and at pH 6.0 collective protonation injects +2/+3 into a low-dielectric interface patch (mutual His-His repulsion + desolvation penalty) that breaks binding. Burial raises both the effective pKa (into the 6-7 window) AND the cooperativity. Test: RFd3 + ProteinMPNN to build the His cluster spanning the interface; PROPKA + a second pKa method on the complex to confirm all His flip-points sit in 6.0-7.0; GFN2-xTB on the capped interface cluster at the pH-7.4 and pH-6.0 protonation states to get a fair dE_switch (per project lesson, xTB not fixed-charge MD); AF2-ig/Boltz-2 at both protonation patterns to confirm the predicted PAE_interaction degrades at low pH. Novelty: moving the PROVEN cooperative network from core-destabilization to interface-destabilization is lightly explored. Risk: medium (buildability of a buried multi-His network at a flat interface).

- **[HIGH-VALUE, NOVEL] Molecular-titration ("proton-decoy") ultrasensitivity to sharpen the transition without more interface His.** Mechanism: add, AWAY from the interface, a cluster of titratable "buffer" groups whose protonation at ~pH 6.3-6.6 drives a small local conformational/electrostatic change that is relayed to the interface. Because a stoichiometric buffer gives an all-or-none threshold controlled by B_T/K_d, a buffer with several coupled sites converts a gradual single-His titration into a near-step. Equivalent framing: engineer the binder so a defined NUMBER of protons must be taken up to release (the Wyman linkage number Dn_H > 1), which is exactly what multiplies the transition steepness. Test: design variants with 1,2,3,4 coupled buffer His; run continuous constant-pH MD (see Q2) to extract the titration curve and fit n_H; the design criterion is n_H increasing toward the >=4 regime (cf. designed filaments at 0.3 pH units). Novelty: molecular-titration ultrasensitivity is standard in signaling but essentially unused in binder design. Risk: medium-high (requires the buffer-to-interface relay to actually couple).

- **[NOVEL] Engineer electrostatic FRUSTRATION at the interface so that protonation flips a minimally-frustrated (bound) state into a highly-frustrated (repulsive) one.** Mechanism: at pH 7.4 arrange interface charges so the contact patch is minimally frustrated (complementary, every charge satisfied); design the His such that protonation at pH 6.0 creates a cluster of mutually unsatisfiable like-charge contacts (maximal frustration) that cannot relax without unbinding. Frustration is a single scalar you can score per residue. Test: compute a frustration index (electrostatic-augmented) on the AF2-ig/Boltz-2 complex at both protonation states; reward designs whose interface frustration is LOW at pH 7.4 and HIGH at pH 6.0. Cross-check the sign of the net force with APBS/PB binding energy at both states. Novelty: using the frustration formalism as a pH-switch design objective is essentially untried. Risk: medium (frustration tools are analysis-oriented; need to wire as an objective).

- **[SPECULATIVE, LOW-NEAR-TERM] Critical-Casimir / near-dewetting amplification.** Mechanism: if the interface is poised near the confined-water dewetting transition, small protonation-induced changes in surface polarity could trigger a disproportionately large (near-critical) change in the water-mediated force, sharpening the switch. Test: umbrella-sample the water occupancy / density in the interfacial gap at both protonation states in explicit-solvent MD; look for a bistable (near-critical) wetting/dewetting response to a small His charge change. Novelty: very high. Risk: very high — hard to control, and GBn2 implicit solvent cannot see dewetting (requires explicit water), so it is a separate validation track, not a screening filter.

- **[SPECULATIVE] Nuclear-quantum / low-barrier H-bond tuning of the flip-point.** Mechanism: a short, symmetric (low-barrier) His...acceptor H-bond at the interface can have a quantum-delocalized proton whose effective pKa and titration sharpness differ from the classical value; this could be used to place the flip-point precisely at 6.0-6.5. Test: GFN2-xTB (or path-integral if available) on the capped His...acceptor cluster to check for a low/flat proton-transfer barrier and compute the H/D isotope shift as a diagnostic of quantum character. Novelty: high. Risk: high — NQE on pKa is a ~0.1-0.5 unit effect, likely too small to be a primary driver; treat as a fine-tuning knob, not a mechanism. Honest read: probably not worth 4-day budget except as a flip-point audit.

- **[NOVEL, MEDIUM] Mechanical/allosteric-network conformational off-switch.** Mechanism: instead of a local charge clash, design a binder whose FOLD is a tuned elastic network with an "allosteric lever": a buried His (the source) whose protonation applies a local strain that propagates via a soft mode to the interface (the target), peeling it off. This decouples the proton sensor from the contact residues, so the interface can be optimized purely for pH-7.4 affinity. Test: build candidate; run an anisotropic-network / normal-mode analysis (or MD covariance, see Q2 FDT) to confirm a low-frequency mode connects the His site to the interface; then explicit-state MD at both protonation states to see the lever fire. Novelty: high (metamaterial allostery is not used in de-novo binders). Risk: high buildability (MPNN+folder tend to produce monolithic folds, not hinged levers — consistent with the project's prior "hinged-lid unbuildable" lesson). 

- **[OUT-OF-SCOPE / HONEST NO-GO] Non-equilibrium & stochastic thermodynamics, PCET.** Kinetic-proofreading-style sharpening and dissipative allostery require a continuously driven energy source (ATP, a redox couple); a passive equilibrium binder at fixed pH cannot run a dissipative cycle, so these cannot sharpen an equilibrium K_d(pH) curve. PCET needs a redox partner/potential that is absent in a plain binder. Report as frameworks considered and rejected for a passive binder, unless the assay provides a driving gradient.

### Gaps
- No source gives a closed-form prediction of the maximum achievable n_H for an INTERFACE (vs core) multi-His switch; the 0.3-pH-unit filament result is the best available empirical ceiling and may not transfer to a 2-protein interface.
- Whether PROPKA/pKAI titration curves are accurate enough to compute a reliable effective Hill coefficient for a de-novo interface is unverified (derivatives amplify pKa error); constant-pH MD is the better-but-costlier readout (Q2).
- No quantitative estimate found for the magnitude of a protonation-triggered near-dewetting force for a realistic mini-protein/TNF interface; the critical-Casimir numbers are for membrane inclusions, not soluble PPIs.

## Q2: How to design a COOPERATIVE multi-site protonation switch (Hill n>1) with coupled titratable residues — coupling physics and how to compute/optimize it

### Takeaway
Cooperativity between protons is an electrostatic-coupling problem: the (de)protonation of one site shifts the pKa of its neighbors, and when the coupling is favorable and burial lowers the local dielectric, the sites titrate together (sharp n_H>1). The coupling is computable at three tiers of cost/accuracy — Poisson-Boltzmann site-site interaction matrices (cheap, screenable), alchemical double-free-energy ddG (rigorous pairwise coupling), and constant-pH MD (gold standard, gives the actual titration curve) — and the whole system is naturally fit to an Ising/site-site model to read off n_H. Electronic-continuum charge scaling (ECC) is the cheap fix for the known over-prediction of salt-bridge/charge-charge energies by fixed-charge force fields.

### Cited Findings
- When titratable sites interact, their titration departs strongly from independent Henderson-Hasselbalch behavior: the (de)protonation of one site shifts the others, so naive per-site pKa values that ignore coupling are significantly wrong — [coupled titratable residues overview, search synthesis](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/resolving-coupled-ph-titrations-using-alchemical-free-energy-calculations/).
- A double-free-energy (ddG) alchemical formalism quantifies the individual site pKa of coupled residues; accounting for coupling can cut the pKa error roughly in half versus coupling-blind estimates — [Wilson et al., Resolving coupled pH titrations by alchemical FE, J. Comput. Chem. 2024](https://www.mpinat.mpg.de/4706156/Wilson_2024_JCC.pdf).
- Tools exist to compute coupled protonation states and the full titration landscape of multi-site systems (e.g., pepKalc for peptides/IDPs) via the site-site electrostatic interaction matrix — [pepKalc](https://st-protein02.chem.au.dk/pepkalc/).
- Charge regulation / protein "capacitance" is the response function of the charge distribution to electrostatic potential and is the key intrinsic property quantifying how a protein's protonation shifts when another charged body approaches; capacitance peaks where titratable groups ionize (i.e., where pH ~ pKa) — [charge regulation & capacitance, PMC5830141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5830141); [patchy/inhomogeneous charge regulation within linearized PB, arXiv:1804.07596](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.07596).
- Constant-pH MD comes in discrete (MC sampling of protonation states interleaved with MD) and continuous (lambda-dynamics, Langevin-propagated titration coordinates, often with pH-replica-exchange) flavors; titration states couple strongly to conformational transitions, and a proton-buffer keeps the box neutral — [Donnini et al., constant-pH MD, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2016](https://www.em.mpg.de/634247/Donnini_2016_JCTC.pdf); [continuous cpHMD, PMC3752133](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3752133); [FMM-electrostatics constant-pH MD in GROMACS, arXiv:2410.01626](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01626).
- The Hill coefficient is formally the slope of the Hill plot at half-saturation; for coupled subunits it is set by subunit-subunit interaction energies (not the number of sites alone) — [On Hill coefficients and subunit interaction energies](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/on-hill-coefficients-and-subunit-interaction-energies/).
- Electronic-continuum correction (ECC) introduces electronic polarization in a mean-field way by scaling all ionic/charged-group charges by 1/sqrt(eps_inf) ~ 0.75; neglecting it makes fixed-charge force fields severely over-bind charge-charge pairs (e.g., salt bridges, ion pairing) — [ECC review, arXiv:2004.07702](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2004.07702); [ECC for ion pairing, arXiv:2308.05482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05482).

### Inferences
- **Three-tier coupling pipeline (ESTABLISHED, directly actionable).** (1) SCREEN: for each candidate complex, build the site-site electrostatic interaction matrix W_ij from a Poisson-Boltzmann solve (APBS) at fixed geometry; diagonalize / Ising-fit to get the predicted titration curve and n_H cheaply across thousands of RFd3 backbones before folding. (2) RANK: on the top designs, run alchemical ddG to get coupled pKa for the His cluster with quantitative pairwise coupling. (3) CONFIRM: continuous constant-pH MD (lambda-dynamics) on the final handful to get the ACTUAL titration curve Y(pH) and fit n_H = slope of the Hill plot; design criterion n_H >> 1 and midpoint ~6.3-6.6.
- **Ising/Potts fit as the design objective (NOVEL-ish).** Treat each interface His as an Ising spin s_i in {neutral, +}, with fields h_i (intrinsic pKa vs pH) and couplings J_ij (the PB W_ij). The two-state partition function Z(pH) gives Y(pH) analytically; n_H is maximized when the J_ij are strong and uniform (low heterogeneity, per the RFIM result) and all h_i cross zero at the same pH. So the explicit optimization target is: tune MPNN sequence/geometry to make the His pKa values DEGENERATE and the inter-His couplings LARGE and POSITIVE. This is a crisp, cheap scalar objective the pipeline can rank on.
- **Burial is the coupling amplifier.** Desolvating the His cluster (low local dielectric) both raises |J_ij| and makes the collective protonation more catastrophic (bigger desolvation penalty when forced charged) — this is why buried networks are sharp. Operationally: add a "burial of the His cluster" reward (SASA-based) to the design score, but cap it so the His still reach a pKa in the 6-7 window (over-burial pushes pKa too low, as in the project's "transition pH shifts to ~3.5" observation and the buried-carboxylate undesignability lesson). Confirm the flip-point with >=2 pKa methods (project lesson).
- **Apply ECC when using MD/xTB for switch energetics.** Fixed-charge Amber will over-count the His+...Lys+ repulsion AND any compensating salt bridges; scale formal charges by ~0.75 (or use xTB, which treats electronics explicitly) for a fair dE_switch. This directly addresses the project lesson that fixed-charge MD occupancy is chemistry-biased. Test: recompute the best switch's dE_switch with ECC-scaled charges and with GFN2-xTB; adopt the switch only if the SIGN and ordering survive both.
- **Score selectivity as a Wyman linkage integral, not a single pKa.** The number of protons released on unbinding, Dn_H(pH) = n_H(bound) - n_H(free), integrated over pH 7.4->6.0, gives ddG_pH = RT ln10 * integral(Dn_H dpH) — the exact pH-selectivity free energy. Maximizing the coupled-His uptake difference between bound and free states is the same as maximizing this integral (see Q1 molecular-titration entry). This is consistent with the project's existing PROPKA-linkage approach but extended to the multi-site cooperative case.

### Gaps
- Continuous constant-pH MD in OpenMM with GBn2 implicit solvent is feasible but its accuracy for a designed multi-His INTERFACE (where burial and partner charges matter) is not benchmarked in the sources found; explicit-solvent cpHMD may be needed for the final readout.
- No source gives a validated mapping from PB W_ij couplings to the final experimentally-observed n_H for a de-novo interface; the Ising-fit objective is physically motivated but its predictive calibration here is unproven.

## Q3: For a flat, polar target with no pocket — what physics maximizes affinity?

### Takeaway
On a flat polar surface you cannot win by shape-complementary burial of a hydrophobic core; affinity must come from (a) displacing a thin layer of unfavorable interfacial water (a controlled, local dewetting/high-energy-water-release effect), (b) building a water-mediated and charge-complementary H-bond network that pays the polar desolvation, and (c) electrostatic steering to raise k_on since you cannot rely on a deep k_off-lowering pocket. The physics that matters most is interfacial water thermodynamics and long-range electrostatics, not van der Waals packing.

### Cited Findings
- Spontaneous dewetting (drying) occurs between patchy hydrophobic protein surfaces at separations <= ~0.6 nm; confined water between two large hydrophobic plates becomes unstable to the vapor below a critical separation, and protein hydration shells sit at the EDGE of this dewetting transition, so binding is very sensitive to small polarity/charge perturbations — [dewetting between hydrophobic surfaces, PMC2861827](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861827); [confined-water evaporation, Berne group JPC C 2009](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/chemistry/groups/berne/papers/jpcC_113_5244_2009.pdf).
- For hydrophilic/patchy interfaces the gap water forms an ADHESIVE H-bond network that can stabilize early binding intermediates (water-mediated contacts are not purely a penalty — they can be load-bearing) — [water-mediated interface interactions, PMC2861827](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861827).
- Electrostatic steering enhances protein-protein association up to ~50-fold; engineering surface charges (e.g., RalGDS triple mutant M26K/D47K/E54K) made binding 14x faster and 25x tighter by stabilizing the diffusional encounter complex and transition state; the effect is ionic-strength dependent (a diagnostic) — [electrostatic steering & association kinetics](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/influence-of-electrostatic-forces-on-the-association-kinetics-and/); [Langevin encounter with reaction patches, arXiv:0809.2871](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.2871).
- Proton linkage makes binding free energy pH-dependent whenever binding takes up or releases protons, because the electrostatic environment of the partners differs between bound and unbound states (Wyman 1964 linked-function theory) — [proton-linked binding thermodynamics, PMC1233671](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1233671).

### Inferences
- **[HIGH-VALUE] Target high-energy interfacial water for displacement (WaterMap-style, explicit-solvent).** Mechanism: on a flat polar surface, affinity is dominated by which water molecules the binder displaces; waters that are H-bond-frustrated or entropically confined on the TNF surface are "hot" and release favorably, while well-satisfied waters cost you. Test: run explicit-solvent MD of apo-TNF (both human and mouse) and compute per-site water occupancy, enthalpy, and entropy (inhomogeneous solvation / grid-based water thermodynamics) to map hot vs cold hydration sites; then bias RFd3/MPNN to place binder apolar contacts over HOT waters and binder H-bond donors/acceptors to REPLACE the H-bonds of displaced cold waters. Novelty: medium (WaterMap is standard in pharma, rare in de-novo binder design). Risk: medium (GBn2 implicit solvent cannot see this — needs explicit water; but it is a target-only precompute, done once).
- **[HIGH-VALUE] Controlled local dewetting as an affinity AND switch lever.** Mechanism: engineer one small, patterned apolar patch on the binder facing a correspondingly apolar TNF sub-site so that the gap water dewets (a strong, entropy-driven attraction) — but keep the patch small so the rest of the interface stays polar/specific. Bonus: because hydration sits at the edge of dewetting, a protonation event nearby can tip the patch from wet to dry or vice versa, coupling affinity to pH (ties Q3 to Q1). Test: explicit-solvent MD with a density/occupancy order parameter in the gap at pH 7.4 vs 6.0. Novelty: high as a pH-switch lever. Risk: high (dewetting is hard to place precisely on a flat surface).
- **[HIGH-VALUE, ESTABLISHED] Electrostatic steering to buy affinity through k_on.** Mechanism: a flat interface gives a shallow well (fast k_off); compensate by making association diffusion-funneled with a complementary long-range charge field, so the binder is steered onto TNF. Give the binder a net-charge patch complementary to the TNF epitope's field. Bonus: the steering field is itself pH-tunable (His/acidic residues), so you can design k_on to drop at pH 6.0 as a kinetic contribution to the switch. Test: APBS electrostatic potential complementarity of binder vs TNF epitope; BrownDye/Langevin Smoluchowski k_on estimate at pH 7.4 vs 6.0 and at 150 mM vs low salt (ionic-strength dependence is the signature). Novelty: low (established), but underused in de-novo binder scoring. Risk: low.
- **[MEDIUM] Maximize polar desolvation complementarity and water-mediated bridging networks.** Mechanism: on a polar surface the win is burying buried-polar H-bonds that exactly satisfy the partner (no unpaired buried polar atoms) plus a few ordered bridging waters that are enthalpically paid for. Test: Boltz-2/AF2-ig interface prediction + count unsatisfied buried polar atoms (penalize); explicit-solvent MD to identify stable bridging waters; GBn2 MM-GBSA as a cheap rank, cross-checked by a short explicit-solvent window for the water term. Novelty: low. Risk: low.
- **Cross-reactivity corollary (mouse).** Steer the design toward CONSERVED polar/charged anchors shared by human and mouse TNF so the electrostatic-steering field and the H-bond network both transfer; avoid relying on a hydration hot-spot that differs between species. Test: map the hydration hot-spots and electrostatic potential for both orthologs and select epitope sub-sites where both the hot-water pattern and the surface charge are conserved.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifies the affinity gain achievable purely from hot-water displacement on a cytokine-flat epitope specifically (the dewetting literature is on model hydrophobic plates/pockets, not TNF).
- The project's GBn2 implicit solvent is structurally blind to the water-thermodynamics and dewetting physics that Q3 says dominate a flat polar interface; every Q3 lever needs an explicit-solvent validation track, which the sources confirm but do not cost out for this system.

## Q4: Which ideas are genuinely novel to the field, highest-upside, and riskiest?

### Takeaway
Ranked for this 4-day-budget project: the safest high-upside move is the cooperative multi-His interface two-state switch (established physics, proven sharp, directly buildable). The best genuinely-novel bets are molecular-titration (proton-decoy) ultrasensitivity and hot-water-displacement affinity engineering. The glamour-but-risky bets are electrostatic-frustration switching, mechanical-lever allostery, and near-dewetting/critical amplification. Nuclear-quantum, PCET, and non-equilibrium thermodynamics are low-probability or out-of-scope for a passive equilibrium binder.

### Cited Findings
- Designed cooperative buried-His systems already achieve near-step pH transitions (0.3 pH units, <1 s), establishing that sharp multi-His cooperativity is physically attainable in de-novo protein, not hypothetical — [Nat. Nanotechnol. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41565-024-01641-1); [Boyken et al., PMC7072037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072037).
- Ultrasensitivity can reach very large effective Hill coefficients (n~10-32) via non-allosteric mechanisms (molecular titration, zero-order), far beyond classical cooperative binding (n~2.8), showing large headroom above a single-His switch — [Ultrasensitivity, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasensitivity).
- Electrostatic steering (established) and mechanical-network allostery (established in metamaterials) are mature in their home fields but not standard in de-novo binder pipelines — [steering](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/influence-of-electrostatic-forces-on-the-association-kinetics-and/); [mechanical networks, PNAS 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.08562).

### Inferences
- **Tier 1 — do now (high upside, low-medium risk, established physics):** (a) cooperative multi-His interface two-state switch with burial-amplified coupling, scored by an Ising/PB coupling objective + constant-pH-MD n_H + xTB dE_switch (Q1/Q2); (b) electrostatic-steering charge complementarity added to the design score, with a pH-tunable field (Q3); (c) Wyman multi-proton linkage integral as the selectivity objective (Q2).
- **Tier 2 — novel, worth a parallel arm (high upside, medium-high risk):** (d) molecular-titration proton-decoy ultrasensitivity to sharpen n_H without crowding the interface (Q1); (e) hot-water-displacement / WaterMap affinity engineering on the flat epitope, with conserved-hydration selection for mouse cross-reactivity (Q3).
- **Tier 3 — glamour bets, only if Tier 1-2 stall (very high novelty, high risk):** (f) engineered electrostatic frustration flip as the switch objective (Q1); (g) mechanical allosteric-lever off-switch decoupling sensor from interface (Q1); (h) protonation-triggered near-dewetting/critical-Casimir amplification (Q1/Q3).
- **Tier 4 — considered and parked (low probability or out-of-scope here):** (i) nuclear-quantum/low-barrier-H-bond flip-point tuning (effect too small to drive the switch; keep only as a flip-point audit); (j) PCET and (k) non-equilibrium/stochastic-thermodynamic sharpening (need a redox partner or a driven energy source absent in a passive binder).
- **Why these rankings:** the project has already shown single-His salt-bridge switches are the modest-but-achievable frontier and that buried-pKa flip-points can be undesignable; the physics says the way past "modest" is COOPERATIVITY (many coupled protons, Ising-sharp) plus a SECOND, orthogonal contribution to K_d(pH) (steering k_on and/or near-dewetting), because stacking independent switch terms multiplies selectivity while a single stronger term fights novelty/buildability.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head benchmark exists (in the sources found) comparing achievable n_H or pH-selectivity ddG across these mechanisms FOR A PROTEIN-PROTEIN INTERFACE; the ranking is physics-reasoned, not data-calibrated, so the Tier-1 vs Tier-2 ordering should be treated as a hypothesis to test in the first design round.
- The multiplicative-selectivity argument (stacking k_on steering + cooperative off-switch) is a plausible inference from linkage/ultrasensitivity theory but I found no direct demonstration of two orthogonal pH-switch terms combining super-additively in a designed binder.
