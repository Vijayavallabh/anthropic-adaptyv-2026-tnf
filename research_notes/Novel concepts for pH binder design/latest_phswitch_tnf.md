# Latest pH-conditional / stimulus-responsive protein binders & TNF-α biology (2023–2026)

> Scope: mechanisms for building pH/conditional switches into de-novo binders (RELEASE-at-low-pH / "sweeping" direction, bind pH 7.4 → release pH 6.0) + TNF-α structural/epitope facts for a conserved, cross-reactive, receptor-blocking epitope. Current as of 2026-10-08.
> Source-quality note: several deep quantitative details of the Baker 2025 paper (exact Rosetta filter thresholds, per-residue IDs, per-target fold-changes) come from automated LLM extraction of the PMC full text (PMC12621740). Headline numbers were cross-checked on two independent fetch passes; where a value is extraction-derived and not in the abstract, it is flagged "verify vs PDF/SI."

## Q1 — Baker lab "Computational design of pH-sensitive binders" (bioRxiv 2025): mechanisms, fold-changes, filters, strongest switches

### Takeaway
The Baker/IPD paper (Ahn, Coventry, …, Baker; bioRxiv 2025-09-29) is the direct blueprint: two computational methods for RELEASE-at-low-pH binders — (1) **interface His adjacent to target cationic residues** (protonation → electrostatic repulsion) and (2) **buried His-containing charged H-bond networks** that acid-destabilize the fold. Reported switches reached **up to 1000-fold** (EphA2), **122-fold** (TNFR2, interface method), and **~79-fold on a de-novo TNFα binder** (buried-network method). Crucially, single scattered histidines do **not** work — ≥2 specified His–cationic contacts or multi-residue buried networks are required, and the **buried-network method is noted to preserve affinity and cross-species reactivity** (directly relevant to our mouse-cross-react requirement).

### Cited Findings
- Paper = "Computational design of pH-sensitive binders," Green Ahn, Brian Coventry, …, David Baker, bioRxiv 2025-09-29; targets stated in the abstract are **EphA2, TNFR2 (tumor necrosis factor receptor 2), IL-6, PCSK9, and the IL-2 mimic Neo2**; fusions to lysosomal-trafficking-receptor binders act as catalytic degraders (LYTAC) — [PubMed PMID 41256377 / DOI 10.1101/2025.09.29.678932](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.09.29.678932).
- **Mechanism 1 (interface repulsion):** place His adjacent to Arg/Lys/His on the target at the interface; on protonation at low pH the imidazole becomes cationic → electrostatic repulsion weakens binding. All pH-dependent interface binders "contained two or more contacts between protonated histidine residues and surrounding cationic residues"; measured His(imidazole-Nε2)–Arg(guanidinium) distances **3.7–7.6 Å** — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **Mechanism 2 (buried charged H-bond network):** bury His forming H-bonds with Lys/Arg/His in the binder core so the fold is destabilized under acid; this method "preserves binding affinity and cross-species reactivity" vs the interface method — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **Fold-changes (pH 7.4 → pH 5.4 unless noted), extraction-derived — verify vs PDF/SI:** EphA2 up to **1000×** weaker (interface; 4 hits from ~12,000 screened); TNFR2 **122×** weaker (interface; 6/43 designs >2×); a de-novo **TNFα binder ~79×** weaker (buried network; ~3/48 designs pH-sensitive at <25 nM); IL-6 **6×**; PCSK9 **3.5×**; Neo2 **>2×** at pH 6.0 (tumor-microenvironment pH) — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **TNFα clarification:** TNFα (the cytokine) is NOT in the abstract target list; the full text applied the buried-network pipeline to a **pre-existing designed TNFα binder** ("We first applied this pipeline to a designed TNFα binder"), achieving ~79× — i.e., the paper demonstrates retrofitting a pH switch onto an existing de-novo TNFα binder, which is exactly our use case — [PMC full text, extraction-derived; verify exact value vs PDF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **Why naive His-bias fails:** initial RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN with global histidine bias (against EphA2, PCSK9) gave more His but "little pH-sensitivity"; they concluded you must specify **His H-bond donor/acceptor status**, not just raise His count — [WebSearch summary of bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.29.678932.full.pdf); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **Computational pipeline / filters (extraction-derived — verify vs Methods/SI):** backbones from **RFdiffusion** (5k–10k/target); sequences from **ProteinMPNN with histidine bias** (settings ~0.5/1.0/1.5); **AlphaFold2** filtering (pAE_interaction <20 initial, <8 after refinement; pLDDT >~88). Rosetta metrics: a **"pH score"** weighting buried H-bond donors/acceptors at pH 7 vs pH 5 (buried core double-donor His weighted far more than surface); a **ΔElectrostatic ddG (ddG_elec)** using only `fa_elec`, filtering for **ddG_elec ≥ 0** (interaction unfavorable at low pH); His–cationic shortest-nonhydrogen-distance counting via rotamer enumeration; buried-network H-bond energy threshold (~< −0.5 kcal/mol vs Gly baseline); **AtomicDepth** for burial depth — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **No explicit pKa predictor** was used; the design relies on His's intrinsic sidechain pKa (~6.5, the only sidechain near neutral pH). Protonated His = donor for two H-bonds, not acceptor; deprotonated His = donor in one + acceptor in one — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **What made the strongest switches:** multiple interacting His (interface designs up to ~8–11 His–cationic contacts; optimum ~2–8); deeper burial for networks; for the top EphA2 design the pH-sensitivity was conferred mainly by **2 histidines** out of 3 placed — [PMC full text, extraction-derived; verify residue IDs vs SI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **Validation assays:** yeast-surface-display titration at pH 7.4 vs 5.5; SPR parallel kinetics (kon/koff) at 7.4 vs 5.4; BLI (associate at 7.4, dissociate at 5.4 — faster koff at low pH is the readout). LYTAC fusions gave substoichiometric/catalytic target degradation, blocked by bafilomycin A1 — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).

### Inferences
- Our current switch (a single interface His next to a conserved target Lys) is the **weak, 1-contact version of Baker Mechanism 1**; the paper's data imply we are leaving most of the switch on the table — their working interface switches needed **≥2 His–cationic contacts** at 3.7–7.6 Å. Adding a second (and third) His–cationic pair near the same target Arg/Lys cluster is the cheapest upgrade and is directly MPNN/Rosetta-designable.
- For **mouse cross-reactivity**, Mechanism 2 (buried intramolecular His network inside OUR binder) is strategically superior: the switch lives in the binder core, not at the species-variable interface, so it does not perturb the cross-reactive epitope contacts — the paper explicitly frames buried networks as affinity- and cross-species-preserving.
- The whole pipeline is **diffusion/MPNN/AF2/Rosetta-native** — no MD or constant-pH simulation needed for the core design loop; MD/pKa tools are only for our post-hoc selectivity confirmation.

### Gaps
- **No Hill coefficient / proton-count / cooperativity value is reported for the binders** (confirmed on targeted re-fetch). The paper mentions a "highly cooperative unfolding transition" only for a 9-H-bond designed homo-oligomer control, not for the binders. So the sharpest-transition claim for networks is qualitative, not a published Hill number.
- Exact Rosetta filter thresholds, ProteinMPNN bias values, and per-residue IDs are LLM-extracted from full text; the precise numbers (ddG_elec cutoff, pH-score weights, His15/19/22) should be confirmed against the Methods/Supplementary before coding filters.
- Whether the 6.0↔7.4 window (our window) alone gives a usable fold-change for their His switches is unclear — most switches were quantified at 7.4 vs **5.4**; only Neo2 was measured at pH 6.0 (>2× only). This suggests the 6.0–7.4 window is intrinsically harder (see Q5).

## Q2 — pH-dependent / recycling / "sweeping" antibodies: transferable design principles

### Takeaway
The antibody field (Chugai especially) established the RELEASE-at-low-pH paradigm years before de-novo methods: **His scanning** in CDRs creates antigen release in the acidified endosome (recycling), and an Fc engineered for pH-dependent **FcRn** binding plus engineered pI turns a recycling antibody into a "**sweeping**" antibody that actively clears antigen. Because broad His scanning limits epitopes and can give incomplete release, Chugai also invented **Ca²⁺-dependent** binding (release driven by low endosomal [Ca²⁺] instead of protons). Principles transferable to de-novo binders: cluster His at the interface, target native cationic/polar hot-spots, and consider metal/ion dependence as an orthogonal trigger.

### Cited Findings
- **Original recycling-antibody concept (Igawa/Chugai):** engineering pH-dependent (His-based) antigen binding so the antibody releases antigen in the endosome and recycles via FcRn to bind again — foundational Nat Biotechnol 2010 paper (Igawa et al.) — [Springer/Nat Biotechnol nbt.1691](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/nbt.1691).
- **His-scanning limitation → Ca²⁺-dependence:** Chugai noted His-based pH dependence "could limit the epitopes that can be targeted and affect the rate of antigen dissociation in the endosome," and instead built an anti-IL-6R antibody that **binds only in the presence of Ca²⁺** (endosomal [Ca²⁺] is lower than plasma → release) — structure **PDB 5AZE**, "Calcium-dependent antigen binding as a novel modality for antibody recycling by endosomal antigen dissociation" — [RCSB 5AZE](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/5AZE); [WebSearch summary](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12060413).
- Chugai's recycling-antibody technology received the **Imperial Invention Prize** (FY2026 National Commendation for Invention) — [ChemXplore news](https://chemxplore.com/news/chugai-imperial-invention-prize-recycling-antibody).
- **SIpHAB (2024, NRC Canada):** a **sequence-based** predictor of His mutations in antibody variable regions for pH-biased binding, trained on structure-based calculations over **3,490 antibody–antigen complexes**; can be parametrized either for acidic-pH-preferred binding (tumor targeting) OR **against acidic pH (endosomal antigen release — our direction)**; needs only the antibody primary sequence; free webserver — [PubMed PMID 39289783 / DOI 10.1080/19420862.2024.2404064](https://doi.org/10.1080/19420862.2024.2404064).
- **Combinatorial His-scanning methods (transferable protocols):** yeast-surface-display selection of binders with strong binding at physiological pH and weak/no binding at acidic pH for half-life-extension/recycling — [PubMed PMID 35482197 / DOI 10.1007/978-1-0716-2285-8_16](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-0716-2285-8_16); phage-display combinatorial His library (up to ~1×10^? members) across a defined sdAb/nanobody–antigen interface, framed as **linked-equilibria / linked-protonation** pH switches — [PubMed PMID 35157278 / DOI 10.1007/978-1-0716-2075-5_13](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-0716-2075-5_13).
- **Stepwise His→acidic-residue mutagenesis (2021):** single-His CDR scan to find ionizable hot-spots, then substitute acidic residues at those spots; best anti-CEA mutant improved pH-selective binding **~32× (ELISA) / ~10× (FACS)**, best in the **pH 5.5–6.0** range — shows acidic residues (Asp/Glu), not only His, can build switches — [PubMed PMID 34956814 / DOI 10.1007/s13205-021-03079-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13205-021-03079-x).

### Inferences
- The antibody literature confirms Baker Mechanism 1 at the empirical level (interface His clusters) and adds two levers de-novo designers under-use: (a) **engineered acidic residues** paired with His to sharpen the transition (Glu/Asp protonation + His), and (b) **ion dependence (Ca²⁺)** as an orthogonal, epitope-agnostic release trigger if a His switch on the TNF epitope proves too shallow.
- SIpHAB's "release-in-endosome" parametrization and its sequence-only nature make it a cheap triage filter we could run on candidate binder sequences even without a complex structure.
- "Sweeping" (vs plain recycling) additionally requires Fc/FcRn + pI engineering — not applicable to a mini-protein binder directly, but the design logic (fast endosomal release + fast target clearance) is the same mechanistic goal.

### Gaps
- No single source here gives a clean, published **Hill coefficient** for a His-cluster antibody switch in the 6.0–7.4 window; the quantitative steepness of antibody His switches is reported as fold-changes, not cooperativity.
- Ca²⁺-dependence is validated for antibodies (anti-IL-6R); no evidence found that a Ca²⁺-dependent trigger has been built into a de-novo mini-protein binder — would be novel and unproven.

## Q3 — Other stimulus-responsive / conditional de-novo binders (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Beyond pH, the de-novo field has a mature toolkit for **conformational/effector-triggered switches** — LOCKR/Co-LOCKR (peptide-key logic gates, Boolean AND/OR/NOT), and (2024) **de-novo allosterically switchable assemblies** built from peptide-switchable hinge modules with **hemoglobin-level cooperativity** (MWC-inspired). These show that cooperative, steep, ligand-triggered responses are designable; the lesson for pH switching is that **global energetic coupling (fold (de)stabilization), not a single local contact, is what produces a sharp switch** — which is exactly the buried-network principle in Q1.

### Cited Findings
- **LOCKR** (Latching Orthogonal Cage/Key pRotein): first fully de-novo protein switch that functions in living cells; original pair of Nature 2019 papers "De novo design of bioactive protein switches" and "Modular and tunable biological feedback control using a de novo protein switch" — [UW Medicine newsroom](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news-releases/designed-switch-allows-unprecedented-control-over-cells); [Bakerlab LOCKR publications](https://www.bakerlab.org/publications/?tsr=LOCKR).
- **Co-LOCKR** (Science 2020): colocalization-dependent switches performing **AND/OR/NOT Boolean logic**, activating by conformational change only when conditions are met; used to redirect T-cell killing to cells bearing 2–3 specified surface antigens — [Lajoie Co-LOCKR 2020 PDF](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Lajoie-coLOCKR2020.pdf); [GeekWire summary](https://www.geekwire.com/2020/scientists-create-proteins-use-logic-choose-cells-kill/).
- **De novo design of allosterically switchable protein assemblies (Nature, Aug 2024):** rigid-body coupling of **peptide-switchable hinge modules** to interfaces drives alternative oligomeric states (MWC model); produces cyclic rings that add/eject subunits on peptide binding and **dihedral cages that disassemble on effector binding** (payload release / drug delivery); **ligand-binding cooperativity comparable to haemoglobin**; allostery arises from **global coupling of substructure energetics** without optimized side-chain communication paths — [Nature s41586-024-07813-2 PDF (IPD)](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/323/93bad0a8e360a61c7ad5034bc5291fb8/s41586-024-07813-2.pdf); [GEN news](https://genengnews.com/?p=299776).

### Inferences
- The 2024 allostery paper is the conceptual bridge to a **sharp** pH switch: couple the protonation event to a **global two-state (bound/released) conformational change** rather than to a single interface contact. For a mini-binder this most plausibly means a buried His/charged network (Q1 Mechanism 2) whose protonation unfolds/loosens the binding-competent conformation — the designable analogue of MWC cooperativity.
- Co-LOCKR/logic shows "release on signal" is a solved de-novo pattern; a pH-triggered "key" (a segment that protonates and ejects) is a conceivable but unproven adaptation for release-at-low-pH.
- CID (chemically-induced dimerization) and small-molecule-triggered de-novo binders exist in the Baker toolkit but were not the strongest match to our pH-release goal; most relevant is the fold-destabilization/disassembly-on-effector principle.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published de-novo binder that uses a **pH-triggered LOCKR-style conformational ejection** specifically (as opposed to the Q1 interface/buried-network affinity modulation). This is a plausible but currently unreferenced design direction.
- No head-to-head data on whether hinge/allosteric cooperativity (hemoglobin-like Hill n>1) has been transferred to a small monomeric TNF-scale binder — the 2024 work is on assemblies/cages.

## Q4 — TNF-α specifics: receptor-binding epitope, critical residues, human vs mouse, known binders, conserved anchors

### Takeaway
The TNFR1/TNFR2 binding site sits **in the cleft between two adjacent subunits of the TNF trimer**; each receptor contacts two protomers. Receptor-binding hot-spots cluster in three loops: **residues ~29–34 (Leu29, Arg31, Arg32, Ala33), ~84–89 (Ser86, Tyr87, Gln88), and ~143–147 (Ala145, Glu146, Ser147)**, with **Tyr87 critical for both receptors**. Receptor subtype split: the **29–34 loop + 86 + 146 preferentially drive TNFR2 (p75)**, while **143–145 mainly drives TNFR1 (p55)**. Clinical anti-TNF mAbs (adalimumab, infliximab) bind conformational epitopes but are **NOT mouse-cross-reactive** — so our conserved, cross-reactive, receptor-blocking epitope must exploit the **conserved TNFR1 (p55) determinants**, not the species-variable TNFR2 region.

### Cited Findings
- **Trimer / receptor geometry:** TNF–TNFR2 complex solved at 3.0 Å (Mukai et al. 2010), PDB **3ALQ**; receptor binds TNF with major contacts at TNFR **CRD2 and CRD3** (cysteine-rich domains), in the groove between two TNF subunits; interface differs between TNFR1 and TNFR2 (basis of selectivity) — [RCSB 3ALQ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/3ALQ); [WebSearch summary](https://proteopedia.org/w/3alq).
- **Receptor-binding residues (mutagenesis / structure):** reduced-binding mutations map to TNF positions **29, 31, 32, 84, 85, 86, 87, 88, 89, 145, 146, 147**; receptor-side contacts place a receptor loop (≈Glu45–Phe49) against TNF **Leu29–Arg32 (subunit A)**, receptor His58–Leu60 against TNF **Arg31–Ala33 (A)**, and receptor **Lys64–Arg66 against TNF Ala145–Glu146 (A)**; receptor Pro12/His55 near TNF **Ser86–Tyr87 (subunit C)** — [WebSearch of US patent TNF-mutant/structure docs](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7732587).
- **Receptor-subtype determinants:** mutations in the **29–34 loop and at 86 and 146 preferentially impair TNFR2 (p75, 75-kDa)** binding; mutations at **143–145 mainly affect TNFR1 (p55, 55-kDa)**; **mutation of conserved Tyr87 causes dramatic loss of binding to BOTH receptors** — [WebSearch summary of classic TNF mutagenesis](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7732587).
- **Receptor-selective TNF mutant (X-ray, Mukai 2008):** key interface residues near TNF **30, 80, 140**; **R31A + R32G** abolish electrostatic interaction with TNFR2; **L29K** contributes to TNFR1 binding — [PubMed PMID 19084540 / DOI 10.1016/j.jmb.2008.11.053](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2008.11.053).
- **Human vs mouse TNF:** mouse TNF-α extracellular domain shares **~70–77% identity** with human TNF-α — [R&D Systems datasheet](https://resources.rndsystems.com/pdfs/datasheets/fab410v.pdf).
- **Cross-species receptor specificity (key for cross-reactivity):** human TNF binds **mouse p55 (TNFR1) but NOT mouse p75 (TNFR2)**; mouse p75 binds only murine TNF; but **human and murine TNF bind almost equally well to the two human receptors** — i.e., the p55/TNFR1-binding surface is conserved across species, the p75/TNFR2 surface is species-restricted — [WebSearch summary of patents/reviews](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8101178).
- **Clinical anti-TNF epitopes:** adalimumab–TNF crystal structure at 3.1 Å, PDB **3WD5**; adalimumab binds the human-TNF region ~**residues 1–36**; infliximab binds the **E–F loop** with contacts scattered over the molecule; all are **conformational** epitopes and **none cross-react with murine TNF** — [RCSB 3WD5](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/3WD5); [WebSearch summary](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7285269).

### Inferences
- **For a conserved, cross-reactive, receptor-blocking epitope:** target the **TNFR1 (p55)-blocking determinants** — the inter-subunit groove including **Tyr87 (conserved, dual-receptor critical)** and the **143–147 region** — because that surface is conserved enough that human TNF binds mouse p55. Avoid leaning on the **TNFR2-specific, species-variable positions (Arg31/Arg32, 86, 146)** as cross-reactivity anchors.
- **Switch anchor choice:** the epitope is **Arg-rich (Arg31, Arg32)**, and the conserved opposing receptor loop uses **Lys64–Arg66**. For a His-repulsion switch (Mechanism 1), **Arg31/Arg32 are the clearest conserved cationic targets** to place protonatable His against — but they are in the TNFR2-leaning, more species-variable loop, so there is tension with cross-reactivity. The buried-network switch (Mechanism 2, in the binder core) sidesteps this tension and is the safer route for preserving the conserved-epitope contacts.
- Blocking the groove (where receptor CRD2/CRD3 docks between two protomers) is the mechanistically correct way to achieve **receptor blocking** for both TNFR1 and TNFR2.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain a residue-by-residue **human-vs-mouse identity map at the specific epitope positions** (29–34 / 84–89 / 143–147). The project's own claim of "a conserved target Lys" at the binder interface is not independently pinned to a specific TNF Lys number in these sources — the cited hot-spots are Arg (31/32) and acidic/polar (E146, S147, Y87), not Lys. Recommend an explicit UniProt P01375 (human) vs P06804 (mouse) alignment at those positions to confirm which cationic residue the current His is paired against and whether it is conserved in mouse.
- Exact atomic contacts of 3ALQ/3WD5 were not retrieved at residue resolution from Proteopedia (page lacked the detail); the residue lists above are from mutagenesis/patent summaries and should be confirmed against the primary structures (PDBe/PDBsum contact tables).

## Q5 — Which mechanisms give the SHARPEST/STRONGEST pH response in the 6.0–7.4 window and are computationally designable?

### Takeaway
Strength and sharpness both come from **coupling multiple protonation events to a cooperative, global two-state change**, not from one interface His. In rank order of designable strength: **(1) buried His-containing charged H-bond networks** (acid-destabilize the fold; strongest demonstrated on a TNFα binder ~79×, affinity- and cross-species-preserving) ≥ **(2) multi-His interface repulsion clusters** (≥2 His vs target Arg/Lys at 3.7–7.6 Å; 122× on TNFR2, up to 1000× on EphA2) >> **(3) a single interface His** (our current switch — modest). The **6.0–7.4 window is intrinsically harder** than 5.4–7.4 because it straddles only the upper half of His's pKa (~6.5) titration; sharpening it needs cooperativity (multiple His and/or paired acidic residues) and/or a non-His trigger (Ca²⁺).

### Cited Findings
- **Buried networks are the strongest single-target result and uniquely preserve affinity + cross-species reactivity** (Baker 2025): ~79× on a de-novo TNFα binder; explicitly contrasted with the interface method as the affinity/cross-species-preserving option — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).
- **Multi-His interface clusters beat single His:** working interface switches required **≥2 His–cationic contacts**; naive His enrichment without donor/acceptor specification gave "little pH-sensitivity" — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/); [bioRxiv PDF via WebSearch](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.29.678932.full.pdf).
- **Cooperativity → sharpness is designable:** de-novo assemblies with peptide-switchable hinges achieve **hemoglobin-comparable ligand cooperativity** via global energetic coupling (Nature 2024) — the general principle that steep switches come from coupling many small energetics, not one contact — [Nature s41586-024-07813-2](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/323/93bad0a8e360a61c7ad5034bc5291fb8/s41586-024-07813-2.pdf).
- **Pairing His with engineered acidic residues sharpens antibody switches** and gave best performance in the **pH 5.5–6.0** range (~32× ELISA) — a transferable lever for the acidic end of our window — [PubMed PMID 34956814 / DOI 10.1007/s13205-021-03079-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13205-021-03079-x).
- **Designability:** all leading mechanisms are built with **RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN (His-biased, donor/acceptor-specified) + AF2 + Rosetta pH-score/ddG_elec filters**; His pKa (~6.5) is used as a fixed value, no pKa predictor in the loop — MD/pKa tools are only for selectivity confirmation, not core design — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/).

### Inferences
- **Recommended design ladder for our bind-7.4/release-6.0 TNFα binder:** (i) cheapest — upgrade the current single interface His to a **2–3 His cluster** against conserved cationic target residues (Mechanism 1), keeping each His 3.7–7.6 Å from a guanidinium/amine; (ii) strongest + cross-species-safe — engineer a **buried His/Lys-Arg charged H-bond network in the binder core** (Mechanism 2), which the paper shows preserves the (cross-reactive) epitope contacts; (iii) to sharpen specifically at 6.0, **pair His with a buried/near-interface Glu or Asp** so two protonation events co-act, and/or stack His to raise effective cooperativity.
- Because our window tops out at 6.0 (not 5.4), expect **smaller raw fold-changes than Baker's headline 79–1000×**; prioritize **cooperativity (multiple coupled titrations)** to get a usable fold-change within 6.0–7.4, and validate the direction/selectivity with explicit-state MD occupancy + ≥2 pKa method classes (per project memory), since static linkage confirms direction but not selectivity.
- If His-only switching stays too shallow in 6.0–7.4, a **Ca²⁺-dependent** interface (Chugai precedent) is the orthogonal fallback trigger, though unproven in de-novo mini-binders.

### Gaps
- No published mechanism was found that reports a **quantified Hill coefficient specifically across 6.0–7.4** for a de-novo binder; the cooperativity claim is mechanistic/qualitative. The exact achievable fold-change for a buried-network or multi-His switch restricted to the 6.0–7.4 window (vs 5.4–7.4) is not directly reported and should be treated as an open design-space question.
- Whether stacking His for cooperativity conflicts with the cross-reactive epitope/novelty constraints on TNF specifically is untested in the literature (project-specific).
