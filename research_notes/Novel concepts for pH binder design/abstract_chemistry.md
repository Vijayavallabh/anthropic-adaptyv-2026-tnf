# Abstract / Novel Chemistry Concepts for a Drastically Improved pH-Switchable TNF-α Binder

Scope: concrete chemistry concepts (including ideas rarely used in de-novo design) that could sharpen/strengthen the 6.0–7.4 pH switch (release at LOW pH) and improve affinity to the flat, polar TNF-α β-jellyroll. Every chemistry is paired with a mechanism, the switch direction, and an in-silico test using our stack (RFdiffusion3, ProteinMPNN, AF2-ig/Boltz-2, OpenMM MD, GFN2-xTB, PROPKA/pKAI, and **JustHISpKa — already in the repo** at `justhispka-1.0.2.tgz` + `wave16L_justhispka.py`). Tags: **[ESTABLISHED]** = demonstrated in literature; **[NOVEL-TO-DESIGN]** = rare/unused in de-novo design; **[SPECULATIVE]** = untested bet.

Grounding fact for the whole file: our current switch (ONE interface His next to a conserved target Lys) is exactly the Baker-lab "interface destabilization" strategy but under-built. Baker's pH-binder paper shows **every** successful pH-dependent binder had **≥2** protonated-His↔cation contacts, and that this scaled to 122–1000× — versus our modest 2–20× with one His — while they explicitly left His-pKa tuning toward pH 6.5 as *unimplemented future work* ([Ahn, Coventry, Baker et al., bioRxiv 2025.09.29.678932, PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)). That gap is our drastic-improvement opening.

## Q1: Chemistries for a SHARP, STRONG pH switch in the 6.0–7.4 window (release at low pH)

### Takeaway
The single highest-confidence upgrade is to stop relying on one interface His and instead build **cooperative multi-His clusters (≥2 His↔cation contacts)** and/or a **buried His-His "monomer-destabilizing" core network** (target-agnostic — ideal for a flat polar target), then **tune each His pKa into ~6.7–7.2 with JustHISpKa** so the full protonation transition lands inside 6.0–7.4. Carboxyl-carboxylate dyads, cation-π switches, buried-charge fold destabilizers, and metal/non-canonical switches are higher-risk amplifiers.

### Cited Findings
- **Histidine is uniquely tunable.** Solvent-exposed His pKa ≈ 6.5; microenvironment can shift it across a span of ~2–9 (up to ~4 units in either direction), making His the most variable titratable residue — the only side chain whose protonation flips under physiological pH ([JustHISpKa paper, Hogues/Wei/Sulea, JCIM 2025, PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)); ([His scanning review, PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)).
- **Burial systematically LOWERS His pKa by ~2 units** (solvent-excluded sidechains, SESA < 20 Å²); JustHISpKa predicts His pKa to **MUE 0.40 / RMSE 0.52 pH units** (R² > 0.75) using Amber electrostatics + continuum solvent (SIE), limited rotamer sampling, proton/Asn-Gln flip optimization, and a buried-sidechain correction ([JustHISpKa, PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)).
- **Anti-Her2 case study (direct precedent for tuning into a window):** His substitutions that switched well between pH 5.0–7.4 had predicted pKa ~5.9–6.6 (center of range); failures were *fully buried* His with pKa < 4.0 (stay neutral, no switch); partial/weak switches had pKa too close to the window edge ([JustHISpKa, PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)).
- **Baker pH-binder quantitative scaling (pH 7.4 → 5.4):** EphA2 up to **1000×** weaker (interface; His15/19/22 near target Arg, up to 11 His-cation contacts); TNFR2 **122×** (interface, up to 8 contacts); **TNF-α 79×** (core-buried His-His network); IL-6 **6×** (His-Lys core network); PCSK9 **3.5×**; Neo2 >2× at pH 6.0 ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)).
- **Cooperativity is mechanistically required, not optional.** All pH-dependent designs had ≥2 protonated-His↔cation contacts; *random* His incorporation failed; for IL-6, mutating either the His **or** the partner Lys abolished pH dependence (direct mutational proof the network — not a lone His — carries the switch) ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)).
- **Two complementary switch architectures** (both give low-pH release): (a) *interface destabilization* — His⁺ electrostatically repels target Arg/Lys/His; (b) *monomer/core destabilization* — buried His H-bonded to cationic residues (His/Arg/Lys), protonation destabilizes the FOLD and raises off-rate. Core networks are described as **target-agnostic**, needing only a hydrophobic core; interface designs "work best when the target presents exposed cationic residues" ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)).
- **Designed hydrogen-bond networks (HBNet) are the enabling primitive.** HBNet (Boyken/Chen/Baker, Science 2016) explicitly searches sequence+rotamer space for self-contained "closed" networks (every buried polar group H-bonded), validated in de-novo coiled-coils; MC-HBNet scales this to larger problems ([HBNet, PMC5497568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5497568); [MC-HBNet, IPD PDF](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/42/89d580f79a3831ed81abefcd08d55d56/rapid-sampling-of-hydrogen-bond-networks-for-computational-protein-design.pdf)).
- **Buried salt bridges are proven pH-sensors that destabilize the fold.** Asp302–His305 in an I-domain: unfolding ΔG drops ~50% as His deprotonates; barnase semi-buried Asp93–Arg69 drives Asp93 pKa below 1; a single T4-lysozyme salt bridge is worth 3–5 kcal/mol of fold stability and is disrupted by pH denaturation; CLIC1 buried salt bridges act as native-state pH sensors ([buried salt-bridge review, PMC3086997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3086997)).
- **"Insert a charge in the hydrophobic interior" (García-Moreno paradigm).** Burying an ionizable group in a low-dielectric core produces large pKa shifts and couples protonation to (un)folding — patented explicitly as a strategy for engineering pH-sensitive switches ([US Patent 10,138,471](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10138471)).
- **Carboxyl-carboxylate dyads give large, tunable pKa shifts.** Two Asp/Glu carboxyls ~2.3–3 Å apart share a proton: one carboxyl's pKa can rise to ~11 (up to ~6 units above its partner), so at neutral pH one is charged and one is neutral; e.g., Cathepsin A Glu69/Glu149 with Glu149 pKa > 8.5 ([Paired Carboxylic Acids in Enzymes, ACS figshare](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/Paired_Carboxylic_Acids_in_Enzymes_and_Their_Role_in_Selective_Substrate_Binding_Catalysis_and_Unusually_Shifted_p_i_K_i_sub_a_sub_Values/8976902); [Li et al. 2004, determinants of carboxyl pKa](https://ns9999k.webs.sigma2.no/10.11582_2021.00081/2016_Tiwari_Reuter_TIMs_PlosCB/tim%20barrel%20pdfs/Li-2004-The%20determinants%20of%20carboxyl%20pKa%20value.pdf)).
- **Metal-coordination pH switches exist.** Hexa-His metal sites (TriCyt / metal-templated interface redesign, Tezcan lab) make metal/pH-switchable oligomerization and bind late-row transition metals with nanomolar Kd; a single α-helical scaffold shows a reversible pH-induced switch of heme axial ligation between His and Cys; His-His + Ni²⁺–Glu coordination builds pH-dependent protein frameworks ([TriCyt, PMC7983065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7983065); [pH heme His/Cys switch, RSC Metallomics C6MT00062B](https://pubs.rsc.org/en-PH/content/getauthorversionpdf/C6MT00062B)).
- **Non-canonical titratable residue with an in-window pKa:** 2,3-diaminopropionic acid (Dap) β-amine pKa falls to ~6.3 in peptides and drives large conformational changes between pH 5 and 7 ([Dap pH-switch vectors, hal-02179234](https://hal-univ-evry.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02179234)).

### Inferences
- **Why our switch is weak — the pKa-suppression trap (key mechanistic insight).** Our design places His next to a target Lys⁺. Two effects *both push His pKa down*: (i) the adjacent cation destabilizes the protonated His⁺ (like-charge), and (ii) partial burial at the interface desolvates the charge (the measured ~2-unit burial downshift). The result is a His that barely protonates even at pH 6.0, so little repulsion is gained — exactly the ~2–20× regime. The fix is to **re-raise the His pKa to ~6.7–7.2** while keeping the repulsion partner. [Cited components: burial −2 units and adjacent-charge effects, [PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/).]
- **How to re-raise the pKa without killing the switch:** place a **second-shell carboxylate (Asp/Glu) or backbone-carbonyl H-bond acceptor on the BINDER** (intramolecular) positioned to stabilize the His⁺, and leave the His partially solvent-exposed. Crucially the pKa-tuning carboxylate must be *intramolecular*, not a His⁺···carboxylate bridge to the target — a target bridge would ADD attraction at low pH and make binding constitutive (consistent with our own MEMORY note that OFF-state His···carboxylate ≈ −12 kcal/mol is constitutive). This is a concrete, rarely-articulated design rule: tune the His pKa with binder-side electrostatics, keep the repulsion on the target side.
- **Adopt the target-agnostic core route for TNF.** Because TNF-α is a flat polar β-jellyroll that may not present a tidy exposed Arg/Lys cluster, the **buried His-His monomer-destabilization network is the better primary bet** (Baker's own TNF-α binder used it: 79×). It decouples the switch from target topology and stacks with any interface His we can place. Build it with HBNet/MC-HBNet constraints inside the binder core.
- **Cooperativity → Hill n > 1 → sharpness.** A cluster of mutually-coupled buried His (JustHISpKa flags interdependence within 6 Å) can titrate more cooperatively than a lone His, compressing the transition into the 6.0–7.4 window and giving a steeper switch than the ~1-pH-unit-per-decade single-site limit. This is the physical route to "sharp," not just "strong."
- **Carboxyl-carboxylate dyad as a reverse-logic switch [NOVEL-TO-DESIGN]:** engineer a buried Asp/Glu dyad at the interface whose shared proton is present at pH 6.0 (neutral, binding-compatible H-bond) but lost at 7.4 (double-negative repulsion). To get *release at low pH* instead, invert: a dyad that is doubly-ionized and interface-compatible at 7.4 but collapses/protonates and distorts the interface at 6.0. Riskier to get the direction right than His, but gives access to the 6.0 edge where His is already fading.
- **Metal-bridge switch [SPECULATIVE]:** a designed His₂/His₃ site that chelates a divalent metal bridging binder→target at pH 7.4; His protonation at pH 6.0 ejects the metal and breaks the bridge. Upside: metal coordination is strong and the pKa competition is steep; downside: requires a metal cofactor in the assay and may be flagged as non-de-novo/non-canonical by reviewers.

### Gaps
- No source gives a *quantitative* Hill coefficient achieved by a designed multi-His cluster; Baker states multiple contacts are necessary but "does not explicitly quantify synergistic cooperativity" ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)). We must measure n in our own MD/pKa titration.
- Whether JustHISpKa's accuracy (MUE 0.40 on natural proteins) holds on *de-novo* interface His in a bound complex is unverified; García-Moreno-style buried-charge pKas are notoriously predictor-divergent (our MEMORY: ≥2 methods disagree 2–3 units on buried carboxylates).
- No direct literature on the carboxyl-carboxylate dyad as a *binder on/off* switch (only enzyme/stability contexts); direction and magnitude for a PPI are unproven.

## Q2: Chemistries for binding the FLAT, POLAR TNF-α β-jellyroll surface

### Takeaway
A flat polar antibody-only surface is the affinity wall, not the switch. The strongest lever is **β-strand augmentation / edge-strand invasion** (RFdiffusion can be conditioned for it, and it was explicitly built to improve binders to *polar* surfaces), backed by **designed water-mediated H-bond networks**, **cation-π to surface aromatics**, and (speculatively) **halogen bonds**. These are orthogonal to the pH switch — design affinity first, then install the His/network switch on the validated interface.

### Cited Findings
- **β-pairing-targeted RFdiffusion is purpose-built for polar surfaces.** RFdiffusion can be conditioned to grow an extended β-sheet that geometrically matches a target edge β-strand, complementing the target's polar backbone/side-chain groups with H-bonding groups on the binder; demonstrated on KIT, PDGFRα, ALK-2/3, FCRL5, NRP1, α-CTX at pM–mid-nM affinity with higher success than unconditioned RFdiffusion, and a KIT co-crystal matches the design. The authors frame it as improving "the design of binders to polar target surfaces" ([β-pairing RFdiffusion, bioRxiv 2024.10.11.617496](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.11.617496.full.pdf); [Nat Commun s41467-025-67866-3](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/365/4c42c439d6300d56d83cbc25f1b2b04d/s41467-025-67866-3_reference.pdf)).
- **Water-mediated H-bonds materially improve polar interfaces.** Explicit models for water-mediated H-bonds plus knowledge of buried crystallographic waters improve ddG-on-mutation prediction and native-sequence recovery in interface design; a de-novo interface H-bond network with second-shell water guidance reportedly reached ≥300× specificity between cognate and non-cognate partners ([water-mediated H-bond design review, Frontiers Mol Biosci 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2018.00088/pdf); [Baker-lab water design, Jiang et al.](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/jiang05A.pdf)).
- **Cation-π is a cheap, abundant polar-interface contact.** ~half of protein complexes and ~1/3 of homodimers contain ≥1 intermolecular cation-π pair (Arg-Tyr most common); average electrostatic contribution ~3 kcal/mol, and such pairs have been placed accurately in de-novo designs (e.g., a designed cation-π against TrkA matched the crystal structure) ([cation-π in PPIs, Univ. Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/cation-%CF%80-interactions-in-protein-protein-interfaces/); [de-novo binder crystallography, SSRL](https://www-ssrl.slac.stanford.edu/content/sites/default/files/documents/science-highlights/pdf/protein-binding_proteins.pdf)).
- **Halogen bonds rival H-bonds in strength.** The σ-hole of a covalently bound halogen forms XBs with electron-rich atoms at energies comparable to H-bonds; site-specific iodo-XBs rescued stability from otherwise destabilizing substitutions in T4 lysozyme, and the cooperative "H-bond-enhanced halogen bond" (HBeXB) is emerging as a design tool ([halogen bonds in proteins thesis, MountainScholar](https://mountainscholar.org/items/90786c38-b68d-4152-a996-2567c902e7b0); [structure-energy of protein XBs, NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10453085)).

### Inferences
- **Lead with edge-strand invasion on TNF-α.** A β-jellyroll exposes edge β-strands; β-pairing-targeted RFdiffusion turns the target's own polar backbone into the binding determinant (backbone-backbone H-bonds are shape-insensitive and pH-robust), directly attacking the "flat polar = low af2ig confidence" wall we have hit. This is the most actionable Q2 idea because it is an RFdiffusion mode, not a new tool.
- **Stack cation-π + water networks as polar hotspots.** On a surface with few hydrophobic anchors, 2–3 designed cation-π pairs (binder Arg/Lys/His to target Tyr/Phe/Trp, or vice-versa) plus 1–2 structured bridging waters can supply the missing ~6–10 kcal/mol that hydrophobic hotspots normally provide — and a *His*-based cation-π doubles as the pH switch (see Q1).
- **Halogens are a non-canonical reach [SPECULATIVE].** A para-iodo/bromo-Phe (ncAA) aimed at a TNF backbone carbonyl or Tyr-OH could add a strong directional contact, but neither ProteinMPNN nor RFdiffusion3 models halogens natively — would require post-hoc mutation + xTB validation and loses "20-canonical" purity.

### Gaps
- The ≥300× water-network specificity figure comes through a secondary summary; the exact primary and its transferability to TNF-α are not pinned here (minor source-precision gap).
- No source quantifies β-strand-augmentation success specifically on a *jellyroll* like TNF-α, nor whether TNF-α's edge strands are solvent-accessible enough for invasion (needs structural check on 1TNF).

## Q3: Supramolecular / dynamic / stimuli-responsive chemistry transferable to the interface

### Takeaway
The transferable ideas are **cooperative/allosteric coupling** (many weak protonations summing to a sharp global response), **foldamer-style pH-triggered conformational change**, and **host-guest-like preorganization** — but our MEMORY and the literature agree that large pH-triggered *conformational* switches (hinged lids) are hard to build with diffusion+MPNN. Treat these as sharpening concepts layered onto the Q1 electrostatic switch, not as standalone mechanisms.

### Cited Findings
- **Dap-rich peptides undergo large pH 5↔7 conformational transitions** driven purely by the ~6.3 β-amine pKa — a foldamer-like, protonation-gated shape change in exactly our window ([Dap vectors, hal-02179234](https://hal-univ-evry.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02179234)).
- **Metal/pH co-responsive assembly is real** (TriCyt hexa-His sites; His-His + Ni²⁺–Glu frameworks) — supramolecular preorganization where protonation and metal binding compete to toggle an interface ([TriCyt, PMC7983065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7983065)).
- **Cooperative H-bond networks are the protein analog of host-guest preorganization:** HBNet "closed" networks lock a defined polar geometry, the same principle that makes synthetic host-guest binding sharp and switchable ([HBNet, PMC5497568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5497568)).

### Inferences
- **Best transferable concept = cooperative allostery for sharpness.** Rather than one bond doing all the work, couple several marginal protonations (a His cluster + a buried-charge network) so the binder's bound-state free energy crosses zero steeply across 6.0→7.4. This is the "many weak, summed" supramolecular design philosophy applied to protonation. [SPECULATIVE on achievable steepness.]
- **Foldamer logic [SPECULATIVE]:** a short binder segment that is ordered/bound at 7.4 but protonation-frustrated and disordered at 6.0 (Dap-like or buried-His-like) would convert a local protonation into global release — but this is close to the "hinged-lid allosteric switch" our MEMORY already flagged as essentially unbuildable (~1/512) with current generators; de-prioritize unless a surface-sensor staple can trigger it.
- **Dynamic-covalent chemistry (pH-labile imine/hydrazone, disulfide) [SPECULATIVE]:** classic stimuli-responsive motifs, but they are chemical conjugations, not 20-canonical, and have no tool support in our pipeline — mention for completeness, do not pursue.

### Gaps
- No literature example of a *de-novo PPI binder* (vs. oligomer/material) driven by a foldamer or host-guest pH mechanism in the 6.0–7.4 window; the transfer is conceptual, not demonstrated.

## Q4: In-silico test for each chemistry (our stack)

### Takeaway
Nearly every proposed test is already scripted in the repo (`ensemble_propka.py`, `obj1_pka_window.py`, `md_pH_swing.py`/`md_pH_switch.py`/`md_release_tnf.py`, `md_cationpi.py`, `md_coop.py`, `ph_release_score.py`, `stage4_ph_score.py`, `wave16L_justhispka.py`). The validation ladder is: **pKa window (JustHISpKa/PROPKA/pKAI) → ΔΔG_elec ON-vs-OFF (Rosetta fa_elec or xTB) → MD occupancy delta (OpenMM, explicit protonation states) → af2ig/Boltz re-fold of the ON state**.

### Cited Findings
- **Baker's own rankable metrics (adopt directly):** (i) a **ΔΔG_elec** computed with the electrostatics-only scoreterm at pH 5 vs pH 7, selecting designs with ΔΔG_elec ≥ 0 (unfavorable when protonated) to isolate the switch from vdW noise; (ii) a **pH Score** weighting buried-vs-surface H-bond donor/acceptor patterns (weights 1.0–18.0× by burial); (iii) **His↔cation rotamer-enumerated distances** (significant at ~3.7–7.6 Å); (iv) H-bond energy < −0.5 kcal/mol for the primary His-cation contact; (v) AF2 filters pAE_interaction < 8, pLDDT > 88 after ProteinMPNN with His bias (0.5/1.0/1.5) or log-prob cutoff −2.5 for core networks ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)).
- **JustHISpKa** gives per-His pKa at MUE 0.40 and explicitly flags coupled His within 6 Å — use it to confirm each switch His lands at pKa ~6.7–7.2 (not <6.0, not >7.4), the documented success window from the anti-Her2 study ([PMC11815838](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11815838/)).

### Inferences (per-chemistry test, concrete)
- **Multi-His interface cluster:** JustHISpKa each His into window → Rosetta ΔΔG_elec(pH5–pH7) ≥ 0 and count ≥2 His↔target-cation pairs at 3.7–7.6 Å → OpenMM `md_pH_swing.py` occupancy of the repulsive contact (expect salt-bridge/contact present at 7.4 state, broken at 6.0 state) → af2ig/Boltz confirm ON-state binding with His neutral.
- **Buried His-His core network (primary bet):** HBNet/MC-HBNet constraint in the core → JustHISpKa coupled-pKa to verify the cluster titrates ~6.5 → GFN2-xTB interaction energy of the capped cluster, neutral vs di-protonated (`md_verdict`/xTB), expecting a large destabilization when protonated → MD fold-RMSF/unfolding propensity at the two states (`md_coop.py`).
- **Carboxyl-carboxylate dyad:** PROPKA/pKAI two-method consensus on the split pKa (expect one ~>8) → xTB proton-shared vs doubly-ionized cluster energy → MD occupancy of the dyad geometry at pH 6 vs 7.4. (Our MEMORY: read SIGN-consensus across ≥2 pKa classes for buried carboxylates.)
- **Cation-π (incl. His cation-π switch):** `md_cationpi.py` occupancy of the aromatic-cation geometry at each protonation state; xTB interaction energy of the capped π-cation cluster. A His-aromatic cation-π that forms only when His⁺ (low pH) would, if the aromatic is on the target, *strengthen* at low pH — so for release-at-low-pH the aromatic must sit such that His⁺ is sterically/electrostatically expelled.
- **Metal-bridge switch:** xTB/QM metal-site interaction energy with His neutral (metal bound) vs His⁺ (metal ejected); MD stability of the bridge at both pH. (High modeling uncertainty — metal parameters in classical MD are weak.)
- **β-strand augmentation / water networks / halogen bonds:** standard af2ig/Boltz interface metrics (pAE_int, pLDDT_binder, iRMSD); for waters, Boltz-2/explicit-water MD occupancy of bridging sites; for halogens, post-hoc ncAA + xTB σ-hole interaction energy (no native tool support).

### Gaps
- Classical MD occupancy under-counts cation-π and over-counts salt bridges (our MEMORY) — the xTB capped-cluster energy is the fairer referee but must be run per-state; no single automated ON/OFF xTB wrapper is confirmed in the script list beyond `md_verdict.py`.
- No in-repo tool models halogens or non-canonical titratables end-to-end; those require manual mutation + QM and break the automated pipeline.

## Q5: Which concepts are genuinely novel, highest-upside, and riskiest

### Takeaway
Rank by expected value: **(1) cooperative multi-His + buried His-His core network with JustHISpKa pKa-targeting** (established, immediately actionable, directly addresses the modest-switch problem and is target-agnostic for the flat surface); **(2) β-strand augmentation for affinity** (established, tool-native, attacks the flat-polar wall); then the novel bets — carboxyl-carboxylate dyads and cation-π switches — then the high-risk metal/non-canonical/foldamer ideas.

### Cited Findings
- Multi-His cooperativity scaled Baker binders to 122–1000× vs. our 2–20×, and His-pKa tuning toward ~6.5 is named as *not yet implemented* — i.e., headroom is documented, not hypothetical ([PMC12621740](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621740/)).
- β-pairing RFdiffusion reached pM–nM on polar targets with crystallographic confirmation ([bioRxiv 2024.10.11.617496](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.11.617496.full.pdf)).
- Carboxyl-carboxylate dyads deliver ~6-unit pKa splits ([ACS figshare](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/Paired_Carboxylic_Acids_in_Enzymes_and_Their_Role_in_Selective_Substrate_Binding_Catalysis_and_Unusually_Shifted_p_i_K_i_sub_a_sub_Values/8976902)); cation-π ~3 kcal/mol each ([Univ. Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/cation-%CF%80-interactions-in-protein-protein-interfaces/)); halogen/metal/Dap switches all demonstrated but only outside de-novo PPI design ([XB thesis](https://mountainscholar.org/items/90786c38-b68d-4152-a996-2567c902e7b0); [TriCyt PMC7983065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7983065); [Dap hal-02179234](https://hal-univ-evry.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02179234)).

### Inferences (ranked portfolio)
- **Highest-upside / lowest-risk [ESTABLISHED]:** (a) **Buried His-His monomer-destabilization core network** — target-agnostic, Baker got 79× on TNF-α itself; (b) **≥2-His cooperative interface cluster** with each His pKa tuned to 6.7–7.2 by *binder-side* carboxylate/desolvation (not a target bridge). These two together are the direct remedy for our modest switch and should be wave-1.
- **High-upside / moderate-risk [NOVEL-TO-DESIGN]:** carboxyl-carboxylate dyad switch (accesses the pH-6.0 edge where His fades, but direction is unproven for a PPI); His-based cation-π that doubles as switch + flat-surface affinity; designed bridging-water networks for polar affinity.
- **High-risk / high-novelty [SPECULATIVE]:** metal-coordination pH switch (needs cofactor, weak MD parameters, reviewer "not de-novo" risk); non-canonical Dap/halogen residues (no pipeline support, breaks 20-canonical constraint); foldamer/hinged-lid conformational switch (MEMORY: ~unbuildable with current generators).
- **Riskiest / likely-avoid:** dynamic-covalent (imine/hydrazone/disulfide) — chemically elegant but not protein-expressible as a 20-canonical de-novo binder; include only as conceptual background.

### Gaps
- Upside estimates for the novel bets (dyad, cation-π switch) are extrapolated from enzyme/stability or non-switch PPI data; none has a published *de-novo pH-binder* demonstration, so expected fold-change is unquantified and must be measured in our own ON/OFF xTB + MD + fold-back ladder.
- No source resolves whether stacking a sharper switch (Hill n>1) against the de-novo *novelty* requirement is jointly achievable — our MEMORY warns sharpness-via-stacking fights novelty, and that a single-His salt bridge may be the practical frontier; the multi-His/core-network route is the best-evidenced attempt to beat that frontier but is not guaranteed.
